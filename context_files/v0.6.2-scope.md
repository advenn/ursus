# ursus v0.6.2 — scope

**Theme: speed and out-of-core, continued.** The maintainer asked for 0.6.2 to be
planned and built once 0.6.1's tasks were done, and named no new theme, so 0.6.1's
stand. Features and I/O breadth are not this release's. Object stores and SQL stay
out, by decision.

**Where it stands:**

- written at step 201, from the profiles that step took of 0.6.1;
- step 202 built D1: a runtime filter tests a bitmap over the join keys' range, when
  it is at most 64 bits a key or a megabyte. q7, q8, q5 and q21 −10 to −19% CPU. D2
  is next;
- step 203 built D2: a join's dense integer keys get a direct index at freeze, which
  the probe reads in place of the hash tables. q17 −12% CPU. Several queries run in
  one of two modes a process, the machine's, not the plan's;
- step 204 built D3: a deferred build of dense keys fills the index directly,
  partitioned by range, with no tables. q21 −18% CPU and −13% wall, q4 −20% and
  −11%. A join's key tables are charged while it probes, which they were not;
- step 205 built G1: a group-by on one String or Binary key keeps its values' own
  bytes and a null placeholder, never encoded. h2o gb1 −31% CPU and −23% wall. gb3,
  100,000 string groups, is bound by memory across eight workers' tables, which
  reading keys ahead did not help; that needs rows partitioned before they are
  aggregated;
- step 206 built O1: reverse writes what it holds to runs past the budget and gives
  them back last to first. 20,000 rows pass under a tenth of their bytes, peaking at
  the limit and part of a batch;
- step 207 built O2: an unkeyed `Rolling` or `GroupByDynamic` hands its windows out in
  segments of a batch's worth, as they close. 200,000 rows answer under a tenth of
  their bytes. Tier 1 is done; the report is next;
- step 208, the report: against v0.6.1, PDS-H SF=1 −8.6% CPU and −2.5% wall, q7
  and q21 −23% CPU and −11 to −12% wall. **The speed claims are met by CPU, not by
  wall time.** Beside Polars: PDS-H SF=1 2.07×, h2o 1.52× over Parquet and 1.65×
  over CSV. Its h2o run was stopped once for low memory and re-run on step 211's
  code;
- step 209 built Tier 2's first item meanwhile: a keyed window's one partition
  larger than the limit streams when its functions are bounded;
- step 210 fixed what step 209 found: a spilled window's merge held its files'
  whole answers, the input, whatever the limit;
- step 211 weighed Tier 2's CSV read by offset and set it aside: profiled, the
  serial read overlaps a parse that needs the time anyway. It made a short quoted
  field scanned once instead: h2o gb1 and gb3 over CSV −7 to −9% CPU;
- step 212 tagged v0.6.2 on the release commit, and v0.6.1 at `f87c31d`, on the
  maintainer's request. Tier 2's quantile over a huge group and chained runtime
  filters are left.

**What they say:**

1. **ZSTD is 11 to 42%, and stays,** for step 191's reasons.
2. **An integer key's hash table is the next largest cost.**
   - q7's runtime filter is 19%: `IntKeyParts.GetMany`, a hash lookup a lineitem row,
     to ask only whether its key exists.
   - q21's build is 45%: `IntKeyTable.getOrInsert` alone is 19%, inserting six
     million lineitem order keys.
   - q17's probe is 17%.
   - TPC-H's keys are dense integers: suppkey 1 to 10,000, partkey to 200,000, and
     orderkey to 6,000,000, a quarter of them used.
3. **A string key's group-by** is two-thirds of h2o gb1 and a quarter of q16, its
   keys encoded to bytes and hashed a byte at a time.

**Out of core,** what 0.6.1 left:

- reverse, hstack and tail refuse;
- `Rolling` and `GroupByDynamic` hold their whole answer until the end;
- a keyed window's one huge partition, and quantile-like aggregates over a huge
  group, refuse.

## 2. Tier 1 — must

### Speed: dense integer keys

**D1. A runtime filter tests a dense key against a bitmap.** S.

- **How:** the join's published key tables build, once, a bitmap over their keys'
  range, when that range is at most 16 times the number of keys. That is at most two
  bytes a key, less than the table. The filter tests a bit a row, and a key outside
  the range is out.
- **Evidence:** finding 2, q7's 19%.

**D2. A join's probe finds a dense key by index.** M.

- **How:** when the build's integer keys span a range at most 4 times their number,
  freeze builds a direct index, key minus the minimum to id, and the probe reads it
  in place of the hash tables.
- **Evidence:** q17's probe, and h2o's joins on integer ids.

**D3. A dense build skips the hash tables.** M.

- **How:** a deferred build knows all its keys before it builds them. When they are
  dense, it fills the direct index directly, without hashing.
- **Evidence:** q21's build. It is profiled after D2.

**G1. A group-by on one string key.** M.

- **Profile first:** h2o gb1's 68%, and q16's.

### Out of core

**O1. Reverse spills.** S. Runs are written in order, and read back last to first,
each run reversed.

**O2. `Rolling` and `GroupByDynamic` with no key emit their windows as they close,**
rather than holding the answer to the end. M.
