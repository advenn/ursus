# ursus v0.6.7 — scope

**Theme: feature completeness, part 3 — frame operations.**

The fourth of the five parts the maintainer ordered on 2026-10-14 for the rest of 0.6
(`v0.6.5-scope.md`):

1. the bugs and stale documents: 0.6.5;
2. the expression model: 0.6.5;
3. namespaces and generators: 0.6.6;
4. **frame operations: this release;**
5. I/O formats.

It takes `feature-gaps.md` §6, what a frame does as a whole: its rows, its columns
and types, its reshaping, its group-by's methods, its joins' remaining options, and
frames to and from Go values. Every name and default below is Polars 1.44.1's, read
from its signatures in the bench's environment, and each item was looked for in
ursus's API first, so the scope lists only what is missing.

## 1. Rows

**R1. `Unique` with a keep.** ursus's `Unique(subset...)` keeps each key's first row,
in the rows' order. Polars' `unique(subset, keep, maintain_order)` keeps the first,
the last, any, or none of a key's rows:

- `UniqueKeep(keep, subset...)`, `KeepFirst`, `KeepLast`, `KeepAny` and `KeepNone`;
- the kept rows in their own order, as Polars' `maintain_order=True` gives them:
  `keep="last"` of the probe frame keeps rows 2, 3, 4 and 5 in that order, and
  `keep="none"` only the keys seen once.

**R2. Duplicates over several columns:**

- `IsDuplicated(exprs...)` and `IsUnique(exprs...)`, a Boolean a row over the
  columns together, every column when none is named. Polars' frame methods give a
  Series; here they are expressions, so a `Filter` takes them. A struct of the
  columns is not a group-by key here, so they count rows over the columns
  themselves;
- `DataFrame.NUnique(ctx, subset...)`, the number of distinct rows.

**R3. `Slice` with a negative offset,** counted from the end as Polars counts it:
`Slice(-3, 2)` of six rows is rows 3 and 4. It is refused today.

**R4. A sample of a frame:** `SampleN(n, withReplacement, shuffle, seed)`,
`SampleFraction(...)` and `Shuffle(seed)` on both frames, the same rows of every
column. The draw is ursus's generator's (0.6.6's §6.3), so the rows are not Polars'.

**R5. Row shortcuts:** `Shift(n, fill)`, `GatherEvery(n, offset)`, `Clear(n)`,
`First()` and `Last()` of a frame; `IsEmpty` and `Equals(other, nullEqual)` of a
`DataFrame`.

## 2. Columns and types

**C1. `Cast` of a frame,** as Polars' `cast(dtypes, strict)`: by column name, by type
(every Int64 to Float32), or every column to one type, strictly unless asked to turn
what cannot be converted into nulls, as `Expr.Cast` and `CastLossy` do.

**C2. `MatchToSchema(schema, options)`:** a frame made to a schema, its missing
columns inserted as nulls or refused, its extra ones dropped or refused, an integer or
a float upcast or not, a struct's fields alike.

**C3. Aggregates of a frame,** one row: `NullCount`, `Sum`, `Mean`, `Median`, `Min`,
`Max`, `Std(ddof)`, `Var(ddof)`, `Quantile(q, interp)`, `Product` and
`ApproxNUnique`. Every column is kept, a null where its aggregate cannot take it, as
Polars answers: the sum of a String column is null, of a Boolean its trues (§7.5).

**C4. Nulls and NaN of a frame:**

- `FillNull` by a strategy with a limit, `fill_null(strategy="forward", limit=1)`.
  The strategies were built at step 249; a limit is per column only today;
- `DropNans(subset...)` and `Interpolate()`.

**C5. Columns:** `InsertColumn(i, expr)`, and `HashRows(seed)`, a UInt64 a row.

## 3. Reshaping

**P1. `Pivot`:**

- **several `On` columns,** each new column named by its values as Polars names it,
  `{"x","p"}`, and `v_{"x","p"}` for several value columns;
- **a lazy `Pivot`,** its new columns given, as Polars' `LazyFrame.pivot` needs
  them: a plan's schema is known before it runs. Today `Pivot` is eager only.

**P2. `Unpivot` without `On`:** every column not in `Index`, as Polars'.

**P3. `PartitionBy`:** a frame for each distinct key, in the order the keys first
appear, with or without the key columns. Go has no tuple-keyed map, so it is a slice
of each key's values beside its frame.

**P4. `Upsample(time, every, groupBy...)`:** a row at each interval from each group's
first instant to its last, the other columns null where no row was, as Polars'
`upsample`, on a calendar as `Every` counts it; and a lazy form.

**P5. `Concat`'s modes:** Polars has eleven. ursus has `ConcatStrict`, which meets
Int32 and Int64 at Int64 but refuses Int64 and Float64, and `ConcatDiagonal`. Added:
a relaxed vertical and diagonal, meeting at the common type as Polars'
`vertical_relaxed` does; `horizontal`; and the five `align` modes, frames joined on
their shared columns.

## 4. Group-by

**G1. `Head(n)` and `Tail(n)`:** each group's first or last n rows, the groups in
their order and each group's rows in theirs.

**G2. `Having(preds...)`:** groups filtered by aggregates of their own before `Agg`,
as Polars' `having`.

**G3. `All()`, and `First` and `Last` ignoring nulls:** every column a list a group;
the first or last non-null value.

**G4. An integer index for temporal groups:** `GroupByDynamic` and `Rolling` over an
Int32 or Int64 index, with Polars' `"3i"` intervals. With it, the rolling functions
and ewms by time take an integer by, which 0.6.6 left.

## 5. Joins

**J1. The remaining options:**

- `maintain_order` of `right`, `left_right` and `right_left`, where ursus's is a
  Boolean today;
- an as-of tolerance on a numeric key, where today it is an interval; as-of
  `coalesce`;
- a suffix for `JoinWhere`.

**J2. `Update(other, on, how, includeNulls)`:** other's values written over this
frame's, matched by keys, its nulls skipped unless asked, as Polars' `update`.

## 6. Go values

**V1. A frame from Go structs:** `FromRows[T](rows)`, the inverse of `Rows[T]`, each
field a column by the same names and tags.

**V2. Rows out:** `RowsByKey`, and `ToMaps`, a map a row, as Polars' `rows_by_key` and
`to_dicts`; and a lazy row iterator over `CollectBatches`.

**V3. An eager `Series` with operations.** Today a `Series` is a read-only view
(`Get`, `Values`, `Map`, `Fold`). It gains the operations a user reaches for on one
column: `Sort`, `Unique`, `Head`, `Tail`, `Slice`, `Reverse`, `FillNull`,
`DropNulls`, `Cast`, `Rename`, and the aggregates, each a one-column frame's.

**Tier 2, if the rest is done:**

- `ToDummies(columns, separator, dropFirst, dropNulls)`, `Unstack(step, how)`, and
  `Transpose` with its names from a column;
- `MapGroups(fn)`, a Go function over each group's frame, and iteration over a
  group-by's groups;
- `Extend`, `WithColumnsSeq`, `EstimatedSize`, `Glimpse`.

## 7. Decisions

**7.1. `Count(ctx)` stays the row count.** Polars' frame `count()` is each column's
non-null count. A Go user reaches for `Count` to learn a frame's height, and §1 of
`feature-gaps.md` names the difference. Polars' form is `Select(All().Count())`.

**7.2. Eager operations take a context,** as every `DataFrame` method does, a
`Series`'s too: each runs a plan, which a context can cancel.

**7.3. A sample's rows are ursus's,** as 0.6.6's §6.3 decided for an expression's.
Unshuffled they keep their order, where Polars' come in its generator's order. Their
tests hold properties: sizes, membership, the same rows a seed.

**7.4. `ConcatStrict` keeps its meaning,** exact meetings only. Polars' default
vertical refuses Int32 beside Int64; ursus's meets them at Int64, which holds both
exactly. The relaxed modes are new names.

**7.5. A frame's aggregates keep every column,** a null where the aggregate cannot
take it, as Polars' do. `GroupBy(k).Sum()` and its kin restrict themselves to the
columns they can take; they are not changed here, and the
difference is recorded beside them.

**7.6. What waits for the engine release:** `profile`, `cache`, `serialize`, display
settings and `show`, `sql`, as `feature-gaps.md` §8 lists them.

## 8. Claims

For each item:

- **it answers as Polars 1.44.1 does,** checked against Polars' answers on the same
  inputs, but where §7 says it does not and §9 of `feature-gaps.md` records it;
- **it spills or streams where its pieces do:**
  - an operation written in frame operators, as its operators do: `UniqueKeep` as
    `Unique` or its window, `Having` as the group-by, `Upsample` as a join, the
    shortcuts as aggregates;
  - an eager one, `PartitionBy` and the `Series` operations, holds its frame, as
    `Pivot` and `Transpose` do;
- **no regression:** the 34 golden plans and the 37 benchmark queries' plans are
  unchanged, which step 287's plan diff checks again at the report.
