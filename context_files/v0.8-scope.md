# ursus v0.8 — scope

**Theme: ursus inside a Go service.**

The maintainer set it on 2026-10-27, as 0.7's performance work ran out of cheap
gains: "the appropriate thing is to raise our head and look for where we are going".
What is left of the distance to Polars on the suites is structural, zstd in pure Go,
text that is copied where Polars' is a view, and each is a large bet for a modest
gain. The README names who ursus is for instead: a Go service with up to tens of
millions of rows, which cannot take on cgo or a sidecar. What such a service feels is
not 1.7 times Polars against 2 on a benchmark, but small queries' latency, many
queries at once, memory it can predict, a query that stops when its request is
cancelled, an API that does not move under it, and the inputs and outputs it has.

0.7 is closed first, by its report, the four suites against Polars, run when the
maintainer says, and its tag on their word.

Each item below was surveyed at step 392, on step 391's code (`16e617a`), on the
laptop, eight cores, at a load of 2.6 to 5 from other work. The probe, `svcprobe`,
runs four queries over a frame of an Int64 key of 100 values, a Float64 and text,
at 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 rows: a filter and a projection, a group-by's sum and
mean, a join to a 100-row dimension, and a sort's head of ten; Polars 1.44.1 runs the
same from Python. It moves into the repository with the first item, as the
scratchpad it lives in does not outlive a session.

**Where it stands:**

- written at step 392;
- step 393 built A1: a goroutine's panic is relayed to the function that started
  it (`uerr.Relay`), at ten sites that recovered nothing, a sort's pieces and a
  unique's parts among them, and `PartitionBy`, `ToDummies` and `Corr` recover at
  their entry; `TestEveryGoroutineIsCovered` holds a WaitGroup's `Go` to recovering
  in its body. The probe is `bench/micro/svcprobe`;
- step 394 built A2, and found worse than the scope said: a cancelled query could
  answer part of its input as the whole, a parallel operator's lanes closed by the
  cancel read as its end, a cancelled count of a CSV file an empty frame and no
  error. The drivers answer a stream that ends under a cancelled context with the
  cancellation; a sort looks at its context each pass, 25 to 49 ms from a cancel
  where it took 410 to 530, and a join's build each batch, 1.5 to 16 where 100 to
  170;
- step 395 built A3, shared by default with an explicit `Engine`, as the maintainer
  chose: a query spills once its engine's queries together are over the budget and
  it over what the others leave it, never less than an even share, and is refused
  only for its own holding. Sixteen queries at once held 1.7 GB of RSS where each on
  a budget of its own held 3.7 to 3.9, the engine's queries 1.4 to 1.8 times its
  budget at their peak. The Parquet page pool, up to 105 MiB a wide scan, moved to
  B3;
- step 396 built B1: where the caller gives no `WithThreads`, a query whose every
  input fits in a batch runs on one thread, a pipeline over at most 16,384 rows
  serial and a group-by over at most 32,768 on one sink, by the sources' own counts.
  The 1,000-row queries take one thread's time on all threads, 0.36 to 0.84 of
  theirs; at 10,000 rows a filter 0.74 and a group-by 0.61; sixty-four 10,000-row
  group-bys at once 7,190 queries a second where 4,280 to 5,090, and 66 goroutines at
  the peak where 750 to 810. A join keeps its threads past a batch until B4;
- step 397 built B2: a query given no `WithThreads` runs on its engine's threads,
  GOMAXPROCS by default, divided by its queries on more than one as it is planned,
  and the eager frame operations and the JSON and CSV writers on their share as the
  rest; `WithEngineThreads`. Sixty-four 200,000-row group-bys at once answer 860 to
  1,000 queries a second where 620 to 850, on 100 goroutines where 940 to 970; one
  alone as before. The Spearman ranks' pair stays two goroutines.

## 1. What a service feels now

### Small queries

The median of 100 to 2,000 runs, in µs; Polars' includes Python's call:

| rows | query | ursus, all threads | ursus, one thread | Polars |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 | filter, select | 49 | 24 | 112 |
| | group-by sum, mean | 87 | 35 | 364 |
| | join 100-row dimension | 98 | 77 | 110 |
| | sort, head 10 | 27 | 23 | 76 |
| 10,000 | filter, select | 178 | 111 | 130 |
| | group-by sum, mean | 276 | 163 | 173 |
| | join 100-row dimension | 495 | 671 | 157 |
| | sort, head 10 | 129 | 120 | 176 |
| 100,000 | filter, select | 726 | 1,062 | 246 |
| | group-by sum, mean | 936 | 1,228 | 596 |
| | join 100-row dimension | 1,700 | 5,343 | 810 |
| | sort, head 10 | 1,183 | 1,131 | 1,326 |

- **A small query fans out anyway:** every pipeline with a batch operator gets a
  dispatcher and a worker a thread (`parallel.go:294`), a group-by a sink and a
  goroutine a thread (`agg.go:988`), a join a key table and a probe worker a thread,
  whatever the rows; only the kernels' own splits wait for 65,536 rows
  (`parsort.go:19`). Under 10,000 rows, all eight threads take one and a half to two
  and a half times one thread's time.
- **A small join is slow on one thread:** a join's build is partitioned, its keys
  integers or packed words (steps 159, 391), only on more than one thread
  (`joinbuild.go:72`); on one, and under a caller's memory limit, the streaming build
  encodes every key as bytes. The 10,000-row join is 3.2 times Polars' on all threads
  and 4.3 on one.
- **Planning is not where the time goes:** a whole 1,000-row query on one thread,
  resolution, eight rule passes (`optimize.go:135`) and physical planning included, is
  23 to 77 µs.

### Many queries at once

The 10,000-row group-by collected by 1 to 64 goroutines for two seconds each:

| goroutines | all threads each | one thread each | goroutines at peak, all threads |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 3,352 queries/s | 3,636 | 73 |
| 4 | 5,381 | 5,808 | 72 |
| 16 | 6,472 | 6,317 | 227 |
| 64 | 8,789 | 8,579 | 769 |

Sixty-four goroutines manage 2.6 times one, on eight cores, at a load of 3 to 4; a
second run of 64 at one thread each, quieter, 13,912. Its profile: 4.9 GB allocated a
second, 1,845 collections in three seconds, 18% of the CPU the collector's, and the
runtime's heap lock contended from `Column.Slice` and fresh buffers
(`arrowx.NewBuffer`). Each Collect starts its own goroutines, `runtime.NumCPU()` of
them by default (`lazy.go:577`), with no cap across queries: 64 at once on sixteen
cores would run some 3,000.

**What a query allocates,** on one thread, a query's average of fifty:

| rows | filter, select | group-by | join | sort, head |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 | 40 kB, 396 allocations | 57 kB, 487 | 99 kB, 509 | 12 kB, 249 |
| 10,000 | 440 kB, 478 | 358 kB, 520 | 917 kB, 621 | 150 kB, 361 |
| 100,000 | 4,202 kB, 1,099 | 807 kB, 652 | 5,791 kB, 1,208 | 1,329 kB, 814 |

The 10,000-row group-by allocates 358 kB to answer 100 rows, and 100,000 rows only
807: something is sized by more than the rows.

### Cancellation

A query over ten million rows, its context cancelled 10 or 50 ms in:

- **a group-by of five million keys** returns 0.8 to 3 ms after the cancel;
- **a sort** 332 to 384 ms after: the in-memory sort at `Finish` (`sort.go:214`) and
  every kernel under it take no context. Nor do the join's partitioned build
  (`joinbuild.go:413`), a group-by's merge (`agg.go:847`), Collect's concatenation
  (`exec.go:52`), or the Parquet reader's decoding goroutines, which stop only at
  `Close` (`parquet/parallel.go:335`);
- **no goroutine is left** after either, and spill files go at each operator's
  `Close`; none sweeps an `ursus-*` directory a crash left.

### Memory

- **A budget is a query's:** each Collect makes its own (`lazy.go:585`), half the
  process's ceiling by default (`execopt/defaultlimit.go:40`), so sixty-four queries
  at once each assume half the machine. No option shares one between queries.
- **The process's limit is set by the first query:** under the default budget it sets
  Go's soft limit to nine tenths of the ceiling (`memlimit.go:55`), process-wide,
  unless GOMEMLIMIT or an earlier limit is set; `LeaveProcessMemoryLimit` declines it.
  A service learns of it from the documentation or not at all.
- **The Parquet page pool** (step 376, `parquet/pool.go:27`) is in no budget: its
  buffers stay until collections drain them.
- **What a large query leaves:** a group-by of five million keys over ten million
  rows, 153 MB of input, mapped 2.1 GB; once collected its live heap is the input's
  153 MB again, but its RSS stays 1.85 GB until the runtime returns the pages, 181 MB
  when forced (`debug.FreeOSMemory`).

### Robustness

Every goroutine the engine starts recovers a panic as an error, the package says
(`lazy.go:684`), and `TestEveryGoroutineIsCovered` holds every `go` statement to it.
`wg.Go` escapes the test: `ranges` (`kernel/parsort.go:23`), `eachPiece`
(`physical/sort.go:311`), `firstseen.go:50`, `textsort.go:200`, `uniquekeys.go:345` and
`aggrank.go:207` recover nothing, so a panic in a sort's piece ends the process, the
service with it. Six helpers size their goroutines by GOMAXPROCS, not the query's
threads (`firstseen.go:50`, `aggrank.go:207`, `frameconv.go:246`,
`framepartition.go:121`, `dummies.go:167`, `jsonwrite.go:134`).

### Startup and size

The package's initialisation, 146 packages, takes 1.8 ms and 984 kB: arrow-go's
`compute` 0.59 ms, `dtype`'s 0.47, its Eisel-Lemire table worked out with big
integers (`dtype/nearest.go:49`, since step 389). A program that uses ursus is
37.7 MB, 26.7 stripped.

### The API and what it connects to

- **About 1,200 exported names** in the root package, and `dtype`, `selector`, `i128`
  and `ursustest` beside it. 0.6.7 listed 19 breaks, 0.6.8 three; a minor version may
  break with no deprecation (`README.md:185`), and no tool checks what one does:
  neither `apidiff` nor `gorelease` is in the Makefile or CI.
- **Errors:** seven sentinels for `errors.Is` (`ursus.go:96`); the structured error,
  its kind, operation and hint, is internal (`internal/uerr/uerr.go:92`), so a caller
  cannot `errors.As` it.
- **In:** CSV, Parquet, NDJSON and JSON by path, glob, bytes and an opener taking a
  context (`csv.go:251`, `parquet.go:271`, `ndjsonread.go:170`), Parquet's ranged
  through an `io.ReaderAt`, the seam for object storage, which stays the caller's by
  decision (`README.md:146`); Arrow IPC by path alone (`ipc.go:40`); arrow-go records;
  `database/sql` read whole (`database.go:54`); Go slices and structs (`FromRows`).
  No `fs.FS`, so no `embed`.
- **Out:** Collect, `CollectBatches`, typed columns, rows into structs and maps
  (`Rows`, `IterRows`), every format to an `io.Writer`, arrow-go records,
  `WriteDatabase`. An `http.ResponseWriter` is an `io.Writer`, and no guide says so.
- **Observability:** `Profile` (step 369), `MemoryStats`, `Explain`; no hook a
  service's metrics could read each query by.
- **Per value:** a zone's `time.LoadLocation` for every formatted instant
  (`dtype/temporal.go:200`) and every use in the kernels (`kernel/dtfn.go:540`),
  uncached; a strftime format compiled per batch (`expr/strptime.go:53`).
- **The documentation:** a `LazyFrame` is safe to share between goroutines
  (`lazy.go:20`); a `DataFrame` has no such sentence. No guide covers a server, its
  threads, its budgets or its cancellation. The README says "one dependency" where
  go.mod has three, and lists SQL, IPC and NDJSON as not done; `ci.yml:13` calls the
  SIMD experiment mandatory where it is optional.

## 2. Safety in a shared process

First, as a service cannot work around them.

### A1. Every goroutine recovers

`ranges`, `eachPiece` and the others above recover a piece's panic and raise it in
the caller, as step 389's `spreader` does, and `TestEveryGoroutineIsCovered` counts
`wg.Go` as it counts `go`. **Measured:** a panic planted in each reaches the caller as
`ErrInternal`. The six helpers sized by GOMAXPROCS went to B2 at step 393, which
decides how a query's workers are counted.

### A2. A cancelled query stops

The sort, the join's partitioned build, a group-by's merge, Collect's concatenation
and the Parquet reader's decoding goroutines look at the context a chunk at a time,
a kernel taking it where it runs long. **Measured:** each over ten million rows,
cancelled at 10 and 50 ms, returns within some tens of milliseconds where the sort
took 330; and none slower uncancelled.

### A3. A process's memory, one budget

Queries in one process draw on one budget, half the ceiling as now, where each
assumes the half for itself; the Parquet page pool is counted in it; and the process
limit the first query sets is a choice the service makes, stated where it is made.
Whether the shared budget is the default or an explicit value a service passes, an
`Engine` of its threads, budget and spill directory, is decided in the step, with B2,
which shares its shape. Decided at step 395: both, the default shared and an
`Engine` a service makes; B2 gives it its threads. **Measured:** sixteen queries at once over large inputs, their
peak RSS and spills under the shared budget against each its own.

## 3. Latency and throughput

### B1. A small query runs small

A pipeline over fewer rows than a threshold runs on the caller's goroutine, and a
group-by or a join over few builds one sink, as the kernels already wait for 65,536
rows. **Measured:** the probe at 1,000 and 10,000 rows takes one thread's time on
all threads, 35 µs for the 1,000-row group-by where it takes 87; 100,000 rows no
slower.

### B2. Workers shared by the process

Concurrent queries take their workers from one pool, or within one cap, rather than
`NumCPU` each; `WithThreads` still caps a query, and the six helpers sized by
GOMAXPROCS take a query's workers as the rest do. **Measured:** the probe's throughput
and peak goroutines at 1, 4, 16 and 64 goroutines.

### B3. What a query allocates

What sizes the 10,000-row group-by's 358 kB, `Column.Slice`'s allocations, and the
buffers made afresh a batch, found and cut. And a scan's page buffers, moved here from
A3 at step 395: up to 105 MiB at once for all of lineitem's columns at SF=1, in no
budget, as a scan's read-ahead is in none (step 214); counted as its engine's
pressure on the other queries, not its own query's, if they are counted at all. **Measured:** allocations a query at
each size, and the collector's share at 64 goroutines, from 18%.

### B4. A small join's keys

The streaming build keys an integer, or packed words, as the partitioned one does,
so a join on one thread, or under a memory limit, does not encode its keys.
**Measured:** the probe's join at 10,000 and 100,000 rows on one thread and all,
from 3.2 times Polars'.

### B5. What is looked up per value

A zone's location loaded once, not a value; a strftime format compiled once a query,
not a batch. **Measured:** a zoned instant formatted, cast and truncated over a
million rows.

## 4. The API a service builds on

### C1. A minor version's breaks, checked

`gorelease`, or `apidiff`, against the last tag in CI, its report the CHANGELOG's
breaks section, so a break is known before it is shipped. ursus stays on ZeroVer, a
stable 1.0 not expected yet; what changes is that every break is listed, by a tool.

### C2. Errors a caller can read

The structured error public, its kind, operation and hint, through `errors.As`, the
sentinels kept.

### C3. A query's numbers, to a hook

A callback a service sets, given each query's duration, rows, peak memory and
spills, for its own metrics; no logging or tracing dependency.

### C4. The inputs a service has

An `fs.FS`, so `embed` and test fixtures read as files do; Arrow IPC from a reader
and bytes, as the other formats have them. Object storage stays the caller's, through
the openers.

## 5. The documentation

### D1. A guide for a service

One page: a `LazyFrame` built once and shared, a `DataFrame` immutable and so shared
too, said in its doc; threads and the shared budget; cancellation by the request's
context; a result streamed to an `http.ResponseWriter` with `CollectBatches` or a
writer; what a large query leaves in RSS, and the process limit. Every example run by
the test suite, as the docs' are.

### D2. What is stale

The README's dependencies and its list of what is not done, `ci.yml`'s comment, and
the README's figures, from 0.7's report.

## 6. Considered and left

- **A server of ursus's own,** HTTP or gRPC: a service has one; ursus is its library.
- **Object-store clients:** the openers are the seam, by decision.
- **A plan cache or prepared queries:** a whole small query is 23 to 77 µs on one
  thread, so planning is not where its time goes; B1 measures it again.
- **0.7's structural bets,** a zstd decoder, text as views, windows over partitions
  at once and `Upsample`'s operator, stay in `feature-gaps.md` §8 for a minor that
  wants them.

## 7. The claims

- **No answer changes,** to the bit where it is a float; the 22 PDS-H answers match
  DuckDB's at every step's gate.
- **Each item is measured** before and after with the probe, at its sizes and
  concurrencies, against the previous step's build and Polars where Polars has the
  same, alternating.
- **The suites no slower:** a step that changes an operator is timed on PDS-H at
  SF=0.1 against the previous runner.
- **Each step:** tests and teeth that bite; the light gate, one package at a time;
  CI's every width, scalar build and race detector after the push.
