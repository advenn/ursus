# ursus v0.8 — the report

0.8's code against Polars 2.1.0 and against v0.7.2's ursus, on the four suites of the
0.7 report and the service probe of [`v0.8-scope.md`](./v0.8-scope.md), every run on
GitHub's runners by `bench.yml` (step 536), none on the laptop.

**What ran.** The library's code is master's `9fd211e`, the merge of step 534, which
v0.8.0 tags with its documents. The runs are on the release branch's `5fbb291` and
`aa38c9d`, that code with `bench.yml`'s changes and nothing else: the probe and the
two runs of 0.8 against v0.7.2 alone on the second. Each run is one job on one `ubuntu-latest` runner, four vCPUs and 16 GB, Go
1.27.0, built with `GOEXPERIMENT=simd GOAMD64=v3`, Polars 2.1.0 installed over the
bench lock's 1.44.1, DuckDB 1.5.5. In each job the engines ran one after another:
ursus, Polars, DuckDB, then v0.7.2's runner, built from the tag's own tree. Each query
ran once untimed and then five times timed at PDS-H and three at h2o, the median kept,
on four threads.

| run | what |
| --- | --- |
| 44797476377, 44797488287 | PDS-H at SF=0.1 and SF=1: ursus, Polars 2.1.0, DuckDB, v0.7.2 |
| 44797500846, 44797512898 | h2o at 10 million rows over Parquet and over CSV, the same four |
| 44798428186 | the service probe: 0.8, v0.7.2 and Polars 2.1.0 |
| 44798333871, 44798346826, 44798358040, 44798372531 | the controls: ursus and the lock's Polars 1.44.1, the four suites |
| 44799194164, 44799210005 | 0.8 and v0.7.2 again, PDS-H at SF=1 and h2o over Parquet |

**How to read it.** A ratio is read within one run, never across runs: the runners
differ, and ursus's h2o geomean over Parquet was 446 ms on one and 670 on another. The
0.7 report's figures are the laptop's, on eight threads, and are not compared with
these.

- **Against Polars 2.1.0,** ursus takes 1.64 to 2.32 times its time. Polars 2.1 is
  faster than 1.44.1 here, most of all on h2o over Parquet: the controls, the lock's
  Polars 1.44.1 beside the same ursus on another runner, give 1.18 to 1.91, near what
  the 0.7 report measured against 1.44.1 on the laptop, 1.2 at h2o and 1.9 to 2.0 at
  PDS-H. So the gap is wider against 2.1 by Polars' own work, not by a change in
  ursus's.
- **Against v0.7.2,** 0.8 takes 0.92 to 0.99 of its time: no suite is slower.
  Run again, 0.8 and v0.7.2 alone on two other runners, PDS-H at SF=1 read 0.99 again and
  h2o over Parquet 0.98. The queries that read above 1.10 once, `q4` 1.25 at SF=1 and
  `gb9` 1.14, read 0.84 and 1.00 there, and others moved the other way, `gb7` from 0.84
  to 1.29: on these runners a query's ratio moves by a quarter from run to run, and only
  a suite's is read. 0.8 changed no kernel on the suites' path; its work is a small
  query's and many queries' (§2).
- **Memory:** ursus's highest peak is h2o's `gb10`, 5.96 GB over Parquet and 7.03 over
  CSV, with no limit set, where Polars' is `j5`, 4.89 and 5.07; v0.7.2's `gb10` 6.51
  and 6.54. A peak with no limit is where the collector lets the heap grow, and moves
  from run to run: `gb10` over CSV peaked at 8.42 GB in its control run. Over the
  queries ursus's peaks are the lowest of the three engines: their geomean is half of
  Polars' or less in every suite, 0.54 GB against 1.08 over Parquet. At PDS-H SF=1 the
  highest is `q21`'s, 0.79 GB, against Polars' 0.65.

## 4. What this report cannot say

- **Four threads, not eight,** and one runner a suite: the 0.7 report ran eight on the
  laptop. A runner's CPU is GitHub's choice, and ursus's figures moved by up to half
  between runners; within a run, the engines ran one after another, not alternated.
- **The other engines** of the 0.7 report, pandas, DataFusion, chDB, duckdb-go, Gota,
  QFrame and arrow-go, were not run: the report is ursus against Polars and its own
  last release. DuckDB ran because it gives the reference answers, and its times are
  its Python process's.
- **Polars' small-query figures include Python's call** into Polars, which a service
  in Python pays; a Go service has no Polars to call without cgo or a sidecar, the
  scope's reason for ursus.
- **Not measured on CI:** a cancelled query's latency, a large query's leftover RSS and
  sixteen queries on one engine (the probe's `cancel`, `mem` and `shared`); steps 394,
  395 and 400 measured them on the laptop.
