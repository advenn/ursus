# ursus v0.6.1 — scope

**Theme: speed where the profile points, and out-of-core.** The maintainer chose both
after v0.6.0 was tagged (step 190), and named the release 0.6.1, not 0.7. Features
and I/O breadth are not this release's. Object stores and SQL stay out, by decision.

ursus stays 0.x, as `v0.6-scope.md` wrote down: an API breaks when the result is
better, and the changelog names each break and its replacement.

**Where it stands:**

- written at step 191, from the profiles that step took of v0.6.0;
- step 192 built P1: the reader decodes a flat chunk's PLAIN and dictionary pages
  itself, levels as runs and each dictionary string copied once. q19 and q12 −22 to
  −23% wall, −10 to −12% CPU; h2o j2, j3 and gb1 −10 to −15% wall. A fuzzer found an
  RLE value wider than its width, now refused. P2 is next;
- step 193 built P2: a row-local conjunct of one String or Binary column, split out
  of the Filter's And, is evaluated once per dictionary value in the reader, and a
  batch's other columns are taken to the kept rows. Reads widen as the filter drops
  rows, after a first version's small batches cost q12 18% of wall time. q19 −27%
  wall against step 192; q8, q10, q12 −3 to −8%. O3 is next;
- step 194 built O3: forward and nearest JoinAsOf stream, queueing per by-key the
  right rows read ahead; the buffered join is removed. Every strategy peaks under
  1 MiB over 300,000 right rows; a by-key the right side lacks still reads it to its
  end. O4 is next;
- step 195 built O4: a pure cross join past its limit writes its build side to one
  spill file and joins each probe row against it, a block at a time, in the
  in-memory order. A keyless join with a residual still refuses. O1 is next;
- step 196 built O1: GroupByDynamic and Rolling stream their input, windows made as
  rows reach them, open windows and a rolling group's rows in reach held, the
  output reordered per key at the end. 120 golden answers recorded on the buffered
  sink match. A dynamic group holds its answer and a batch (7.5 → 0.4 MB over 300k
  rows); a rolling group holds its answer, a row an input row (17 → 10 MB): it is
  under a fifth of its input only when its answer is. Streaming the output out is
  left for later. O2 is next;
- step 197 built O2 for a window with no partition_by: past its limit its rows go
  to one file and are read back a batch at a time, aggregates broadcast, cumulative
  functions and an unlimited forward fill carrying their last answer, and shifts,
  limited fills and rolling windows of rows run over the batch with a bounded
  context. Rank, ewm, interpolation, ordered windows and one huge keyed partition
  still refuse. The out-of-core items are done; P3 and P4 are next.

**What they say:**

1. **Reading Parquet is most of every wide gap:** 48 to 77% of the PDS-H queries
   above.
2. **ZSTD is 17 to 48% of them, and stays.** It runs klauspost's decoder, whose fast
   loop is disabled (`const useSafe = true`, after crashes found by fuzzing). A pure
   Go reader has no faster one. And no page can be skipped without a page index,
   which PDS-H's files lack, and whose filtered rows are scattered anyway.
3. **A dictionary string is copied twice:** arrow-go's `dictConverter` copies each
   value's slice header out of the dictionary, and `byteArrayCol` then copies its
   bytes. That is 25 to 31% of q19, q12, j3 and j2.
4. **A string filter compares every row:** `IsIn` and `==` on strings are 21 to 22%
   of q19 and q12. Those columns are dictionary-encoded, with a handful of distinct
   values.
5. **Definition levels are 3 to 10%:** arrow-go decodes them into an `int16` a row,
   even for a column without a null.
6. **A join's output is gathered by row:** probe and `Take` are 27 to 37% of j2 and
   j3.

**Out of core,** the operators that do not spill, from 0.6.0's known limitations:

- `Rolling` and `GroupByDynamic` buffer their input, though they require it sorted;
- a window with no `partition_by`, or one partition larger than the limit, refuses;
- `JoinAsOf` forward and nearest buffer the right side;
- a cross join buffers its build side;
- reverse, tail and hstack;
- quantile-like aggregates over a few very large groups.

## 2. Tier 1 — must

### Speed

**P1. The Parquet reader decodes its own pages.** L.

- **How:** arrow-go's public `GetColumnPageReader` yields each column chunk's pages,
  decompressed. ursus decodes them itself:
  - definition levels, as RLE and bit-packed runs: a run of present values sets a
    run of validity bits, with nothing a row;
  - PLAIN values, and RLE_DICTIONARY indices into the chunk's dictionary;
  - a dictionary string, gathered from the dictionary page straight into the String
    layout, copied once.
- **The rest stays arrow-go's:** a chunk with another encoding (DELTA_*,
  BYTE_STREAM_SPLIT) or nesting reads through `ColumnChunkReader`, as now. The
  chunk's encoding statistics decide before the first page.
- **Evidence:** finding 3 and finding 5: 25 to 31% of four queries, and 3 to 10% of
  all of them.
- **Tests:** every file the reader's tests read, through both paths, compared; a
  fuzzer over the RLE and bit-packed hybrid; files written by arrow-go, DuckDB and
  Polars, nulls and all.

**P2. A string predicate on a dictionary column runs once per dictionary entry.** L.

- **How:** the scan is given the conjuncts the planner pushes down to it. One that
  reads a single dictionary-encoded String column is evaluated in the reader, per
  row group:
  - the conjuncts are `==`, `!=`, `IsIn`, `StartsWith`, `EndsWith`, `Contains` and
    the literal regexps of step 169;
  - each runs on the dictionary's values once, then is looked up by each row's
    index;
  - a row it drops is never gathered, in any column.
- **The filter above stays:** it reapplies the conjunct to rows that already pass,
  which is cheap and keeps one answer if the reader declines.
- **Evidence:** finding 4: 21 to 22% of q19 and q12, on top of P1's share.
- **Tests:**
  - answers with the rule on and off;
  - a dictionary that changes between row groups;
  - a column whose later pages fall back from dictionary to PLAIN, as writers do when
    a dictionary grows too large;
  - nulls.

**P3. A join's output gathered once.** L.

- **How:** the probe emits the match pairs of a batch, and each output column is
  gathered from them once, at the end, rather than appended to row by row.
- **Evidence:** finding 6.
- **Profile first:** `Take` and the probe's appends are measured separately before
  the step commits to a design.

**P4. The parallel CSV splitter.** M.

- **How:** the splitter cuts blocks on one goroutine, and it is the bound
  (`v0.6-scope.md` Tier 2). Blocks are cut where a record surely starts, found by
  several goroutines at once, with the quote state settled by a second pass.
- **Evidence:** h2o over CSV is 1.8× Polars, from every query's reading. The step
  profiles gb1 over CSV first.

### Out of core

**O1. `Rolling` and `GroupByDynamic` stream.** L.

- **How:** the index is sorted, so a window is a range of rows.
  - The operator holds only the rows from the earliest open window's start.
  - It emits each window once the index passes its end.
  - With keys, it holds each key's rows of its open windows.
- **Output order:** the output is per key. If streaming changes that order, the
  windows are written to a spill file per key and read back in order, or the order
  is made the stream's and the change listed.
- **Tests:** answers against the buffered operator; an input ten times the budget,
  under it; the empty windows the grid makes.

**O2. A window over one partition larger than the budget.** L.

- **How:**
  - The functions that read rows in order (cumulative, `Shift`, `Diff`, rolling by
    rows, ewm) stream, carrying their state from batch to batch.
  - An aggregate broadcast over the partition, such as `x - x.Mean().Over(nothing)`,
    spills its input, computes the aggregate, and reads the input again.
- **Tests:** each function, with no `partition_by` and with one huge partition,
  against the answer without a budget.

**O3. `JoinAsOf` forward and nearest stream.** M.

- **How:** backward streams since step 183. Forward and nearest read the right side
  in order:
  - each by-key holds the right rows its left rows have not passed;
  - a by-key whose left rows run ahead of its right rows reads the right side on;
  - what is held is charged, and spilled when over.
- **Tests:** step 183's tests, for forward and nearest; a right side ten times the
  budget.

**O4. A cross join over its budget.** M.

- **How:** a block nested loop. The build side is written to disk in blocks that fit,
  and the probe side, spilled once, is read once a block.
- **Tests:** answers against the in-memory join; a build side three times the budget.

### The record

- **The baseline:** `runner-v060`, v0.6.0's code built with the bench's flags, is
  each speed step's comparison, alternated in one session.
- **Benchmarks:** each step times one query at a time. One full report at the end.

## 4. Tier 3 — later, each with its reason

| item | why not in 0.6.1 |
| --- | --- |
| a String column that refers to its dictionary | XL: a new storage form behind about forty string call sites. P2 takes its filter win inside the reader. |
| a faster ZSTD | no pure Go decoder is faster, and cgo is out |
| page-index pruning | PDS-H's files carry no page index |
| features, I/O breadth | not this release's, by the maintainer's choice |

## 5. What 0.6.1 claims

> **q19 and q12 are at least 30% faster than v0.6.0, and PDS-H at SF=1 at least 10%
> faster by geomean, each alternated in one session. `Rolling`, `GroupByDynamic`, a
> window over one partition, a forward or nearest `JoinAsOf` and a cross join each
> pass under a budget a tenth of their input, answering as they do without one.**
