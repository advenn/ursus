# ursus v0.6 — scope

**Theme: speed where the profile points, the features 0.5 left, and robustness.**
The maintainer chose all three after v0.5.0 was tagged (step 155). Object stores and
SQL stay out, by decision. I/O breadth — NDJSON, Arrow IPC files, hive partitioning —
is not this release's.

**ursus will stay 0.x.** It will never reach 1.0, so nothing here is planned toward
stability: no deprecation periods, no freeze, no compatibility shims. An API breaks
when the result is better, and the changelog names each break and its replacement.
Anything that waited on "the API settling" is decided now instead.

### Speed

**0. Profile and baseline first.** S.

- **Profiles:** CPU profiles at SF=1 of q4 and q16, never profiled, and of q2, q7,
  q12, q17 and q19 again, on v0.5.0. One query at a time, by the laptop rule.
  Summaries recorded, as step 141 did.
- **Baseline:** ursus and Polars in one session, so the end has no drift to explain.
- **Then:** items 1 to 7 are re-ranked by what the profiles show.

**1. The reader's double copies.** S each.

- **Strings:** `byteArrayCol.finish` builds the column through `NewStringParts`,
  which allocates and copies offsets and characters a second time
  (`internal/source/parquet/column.go:576-580`). It is steps 136–138's mistake, for
  strings. Handing the buffers over with `NewStringBuffers` copies nothing.
- **Fixed width:** `finish` wraps `c.out` with `NewFixed`, which copies
  (`column.go:310-321`). Reading into a fresh buffer each batch and wrapping it with
  `NewFixedBuffer` does not.
- **The test:** the allocation test (`alloc_internal_test.go`) reads Int64 only, so it
  gains a String column.

**2. Predicate kernels.** S each.

- **A top-level `And`, split.** `IsBetween` is `Ge(…).And(Le(…))` (`expr.go:427-450`),
  so both comparisons run over the whole batch and are combined three-valued.
  Splitting a filter's top-level `And` into conjuncts runs the second on the first's
  survivors. q4–q7, q10, q12, q14, q15 and q20 filter by ranges.
- **AND and OR a word at a time** when neither side has nulls. Today they go a bit at
  a time, with closures (`internal/kernel/dispatch.go:358-406`). Step 141 put them at
  12% of q15, and q19's OR of ANDs is eleven of them over about 430k rows.
- **A typed integer `InSet`,** without encoding each row to a map key
  (`internal/kernel/select.go:146-200`). q16's `p_size`.

**3. Join sides chosen by what survives.** S–M.

- **The fault.** `EstimateRows` ignores filters (`internal/plan/rule_buildside.go:271`),
  and a join swaps when its right side is estimated at twice its left. q12 builds
  orders' 1.5M keys to probe about 31k filtered lineitem rows.
- **The fix:** compare both inputs' actual sizes before the build, adaptively; or
  apply a selectivity factor, which is S and a heuristic. Item 0 decides which.
  Every swap it changes is checked against PDS-H's answers.
- **A parallel build for semi and anti joins.** `deferBuild` refuses a join that keeps
  no build rows (`internal/physical/joinbuild.go:51-61`). So q4 and q22 build 1.4M and
  1.5M keys serially, though their right side is one key column, 8 bytes a row.

**4. A single-integer-key path.** M, and S to share it with the group-by.

- **Today:** every key goes through `GroupKeyEncoder` to 9 bytes, with a closure per
  row, then a hash that loops byte by byte, then `bytes.Equal` through the arena
  (`internal/kernel/groupkey.go`, `keytable.go`; `internal/physical/keychunk.go`).
- **The change:** a table of int64 keys read straight from the column, hashed with
  `Mix64`, keeping the batched slot reads. It needs integer methods on `joinKeys`
  and `KeyParts`. Spill routes by encoded bytes, so it encodes only when routing.
- **Reaches:**
  - q2, q17 and q16's lookups;
  - q12's, q4's and q22's builds;
  - q7's probes;
  - single-integer group-bys: q13, q17, q18.

  q5, q9 and q20's composite keys stay on the byte path.

**5. q19's implied predicates.** S–M.

- **The fault.** q19 joins lineitem to all 200k parts, gathers p_brand and
  p_container for about 430k pairs, and only then applies its OR of three ANDs,
  leaving a few hundred rows. Pushdown moves single-side conjuncts only
  (`internal/plan/rule_predicate.go:611-678`).
- **The change:** from an OR whose every arm constrains one side, derive that side's
  implied predicate, and push it down while keeping the OR. For q19:
  - `p_brand` in 3 values;
  - `p_container` in 12;
  - `p_size` between 1 and 15;
  - `l_quantity` between 1 and 30.

  The part build should fall to about 2k rows.
- **Care:** a derived predicate must be implied, never a guess, and `holdFallible`'s
  ordering rules apply.

**6. Runtime filters, one hop.** M.

- **Why it works:** a join's build finishes before its probe is pulled
  (`internal/physical/join.go:76-97`).
- **The change:** the build's key set — exact when small, a bloom filter otherwise —
  goes in where the probe's key column flows unchanged from its scan.
- **For q2:** part's ~750 keys would cut partsupp from 800k rows to about 3k before
  they probe the European suppliers.
- **Not here:** chaining through build sides, which q7 and q21 need, is Tier 2.

**7. A probe that copies only what it must.** S.

- When every probe row matched exactly once, in order, the probe columns are reused,
  as the semi path already does (`join.go:1411`).
- Rows that missed are passed over without `enter`.

**Found during the steps:**

- **The miss path** (step 157, built at step 158). S.
- **`LIKE` with two wildcards runs as a regular expression** (step 160). q13's
  `o_comment NOT LIKE '%special%requests%'` goes through Go's backtracking regexp,
  about 18% of q13's CPU. A pattern of literals between `%`s is a sequence of
  substring searches. S.
- **`First` and `Last` keep a one-row column per group** (step 160). Each batch
  builds a map and a `Take` per group, `NBytes` walks every group, and `Finish`
  concatenates one part per group. Over 182k groups `First` took 0.8 s and `Last`
  1.3 s, where `Sum` took 30 ms. The other accumulators keep typed per-group state.
  S–M.

**The target, in two parts:**

- PDS-H SF=1 within 3× of Polars, in one session;
- ursus's own SF=1 geomean at least 20% below v0.5.0's, the two alternated in one
  session. Polars' drift cannot move this one.

### Features: what 0.5 left

**F1. Two-input aggregates: `Corr`, `Cov`, `MinBy`, `MaxBy`.** M.

- **How:** an `expr.Agg` has one child, so the two inputs go in as step 146's `Struct`.
  The node keeps its shape, and the accumulator reads the struct's two fields.
- **Merging:** Chan's formulas for the moments, so each runs in parallel, as the
  others do.

**F2. `ReplaceTimeZone`.** M.

- **Built on:** step 145's `wallTime`.
- **Policies,** as options: Polars' `ambiguous` (raise, earliest, latest, null) and
  `non_existent` (raise, null).

**F3. Rolling windows by time.** M. `Rolling*By(index, period)`, the expression form
of the frame-level `lf.Rolling` (`groupby.go:100`), whose per-row windows it reuses.

**F4. The leftovers, each S, each built or closed with its reason.** The v0.4 scope
moved several of them to 0.5, which dropped them without one.

- `Interpolate`'s nearest method;
- rolling `center=True`;
- Decimal `//`, `%` and `**` (`internal/expr/resolve.go:211-214`);
- the Datetime mean;
- Parquet's UUID and JSON logical types (`internal/source/parquet/types.go:145`).

**F5. `WithFields`.** S, now that step 146's horizontal calls exist.

### Robustness

**R1. A streaming `MergeSorted`, and a streaming `JoinAsOf`.**

- `MergeSorted` is S–M: it streams through `kernel/merge.go`'s merger, and then
  needs no spill.
- `JoinAsOf` is M.
- Both hold their inputs today, bounded and charged.

**R2. The open audit rows,** each fixed or closed with a decision:

- **S26:** `MinInt // -1` wraps; refuse it, as Int128 arithmetic refuses.
- **S21:** `ToUpper("ß")` is `"ß"`.
- **I26:** `DataFrame.Rows` does not decode a List.
- **O13:** two Enums or two Structs rendering alike; measure it.
- **I23:** a third-party file without null counts can be pruned wrongly by `IsNull`;
  read the statistics conservatively.
- **O12:** the optimizer can turn a runtime error into a result. Decide the contract
  and write it down.
- **J11:** one step, time-boxed. If the cause is still not found, the record says
  what was ruled out.

**R3. A default budget off Linux.** S–M.

- On macOS and Windows a query is unbudgeted, and no `GOMEMLIMIT` is set
  (`internal/execopt/defaultlimit.go:33-34`).
- The v0.5 changelog's limitations do not say so.

**R4. 0.5's new expressions, against an outside oracle.** M.

- **Today:** none of them runs through a validated suite; steps 145–153 rely on
  in-repo oracles and hand values.
- **The change:** a differential suite whose expected answers come from Polars,
  produced once by the bench's own Python environment and checked in.
- **It covers:** the calendar, rolling, ewm, `Cut` and `QCut`, `Describe`,
  `ValueCounts`, `ConcatStr` and the bit counts.

**R5. The memory test gaps** steps 131, 135 and 138 name. Each is built if it can be
made to bite within a step, and otherwise closed for good, with that reason.

### API breaks worth making

S–M each, as the steps reach them:

- **`DynamicOptions.Label`, `StartBy` and `IncludeBoundaries`** exist only to be
  refused (`groupby.go:44-48`). Build them or remove them.
- **`WithOptFlags(plan.Flags)`** puts an internal type in the public API
  (`lazy.go:247`). Give it a public options type, or remove it.
- **The rest of the internal types in the public API,** found by the midpoint audit
  (`audit-0.6-midpoint.md` §4.4): `Scan(plan.Source)`, `FromPlan`, `LazyFrame.Plan`,
  `DataFrame.Batch`, and option types a caller cannot construct. Unexport each, or
  give it a public type.
- **Others the steps find,** each in the changelog's breaks.

## 4. Tier 3 — later, each with its reason

## 5. What 0.6 claims

> **ursus is within 3× of Polars on PDS-H at SF=1, measured in one session, and at
> least 20% faster than v0.5.0 on it; and it has what 0.5 left.**

The features are done when they are built and tested. The robustness items are done
when each is fixed, or closed with a decision the record gives.

## 6. Order

1. **Step 156:** this scope, with H1 and H2.
2. **Item 0:** the profiles and the baseline; re-rank items 1–7.
3. **Items 1 and 2:** small and independent.
4. **Items 3 and 4, then 5, 6 and 7,** in the profile's order.
5. **F1–F5 and R1–R5,** between the speed steps; they share little code with them.
6. **The API breaks,** as each is reached.
7. **The report, then the tag,** on request.

**Each step keeps the rhythm:**

- evidence first;
- the fix;
- teeth that bite;
- the full gate, and PDS-H SF=0.1's answers wherever a step touches plans or joins;
- an as-built record.

Benchmarks stay targeted, one query at a time.
