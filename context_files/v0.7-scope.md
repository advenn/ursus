# ursus v0.7 — scope

**Theme: performance.**

The maintainer set it on 2026-10-14, one theme a minor version: 0.6 was feature
completeness, 0.7 is speed. On 2026-10-26, tagging v0.6.8, they said: "start 0.7,
scope it first". 0.6.8 had already done five of 0.7's tasks: `Profile`, a count that
reads no column, slice pushdown, a frame's aggregate taken whole and a faster CSV read.

Each item below was surveyed at step 375, on v0.6.8's code (`411a081`). The file and
line references are that survey's, and so are the measurements: on the laptop, at a
load of 3 to 4 from other work, both engines on the same threads.

**Where it stands:**

- written at step 375;
- step 376 built R1's page buffers: a pooled allocator arrow-go's page readers
  decompress into, so a scan allocates 2.2 GB in seven runs of q14's columns where it
  allocated 3.9; PDS-H's Parquet-bound queries 0.91 to 0.95 of their time. The
  buffered stream, and with it `GetStream`'s copy of each chunk, is left: it lost two
  refusals two tests hold;
- step 377 built R2, narrower: the columns beside a text predicate the reader judges
  are read at its kept rows, a PLAIN value loaded where it lies, a dictionary index
  looked up only where kept, a string copied only where kept; q10 0.82 and q19 0.84
  of their time at SF=1. Judging every column's conjuncts in the reader was built and
  measured slower, q19 1.20 and q6 1.13: the Filter above judged them cheaply, and
  a page a column chunk leaves no decompression to skip;
- step 378 built R3's text half: a dictionary page of present rows marked in two
  plain loops, and text emitted by the kept rows' positions; q12 0.92 and q10 0.94
  of step 377's time. The indices' unpacking is left as step 192 made it.

## 1. Where the time goes

times Polars' time, at SF=0.1 2.3; h2o over Parquet 1.3 and over CSV 1.6. Its worst
queries, timed again at this step on four threads, alternating with Polars 1.44.1, two
rounds of five:

| q19 | 272 | 90 | 3.0 |

**The bare scan.** The four columns q14 reads, summed, on one thread: ursus 306–326 ms,
Polars 127–133; one column, `l_extendedprice`, 108–134 against 50. Both read the
same pages: DuckDB wrote lineitem in 51 row groups of a page a column, and no row
group's statistics exclude q14's month, so every page is decompressed by both.

- **zstd is a floor, but not the whole gap.** klauspost's decoder, the pure-Go one
  arrow-go uses, takes lineitem's two widest columns at 508–571 MB/s on one thread;
  libzstd, through pyarrow, at 849; Polars sums a column at about 1 GB/s with its
  decode. The decoder is no faster in its other modes: its fast copy is off in its
  source (`seqdec_asm.go:91`, `const useSafe = true`). ursus is pure Go, so this
  stays: on q14's scan about 210 ms of zstd a run against Polars' 110 or so.
- **What is ursus's:** about 100 ms of the scan besides zstd, where Polars takes
  15 to 20. Of the scan's profile, 29% is the runtime's: collections, zeroing and
  copying of buffers allocated afresh for each page and batch. A page reader is made
  each column chunk (`parquet.go:1135`) with buffers of its own (arrow-go's
  `page_reader.go:515`), so every page of a megabyte is a new allocation.
- **What is done already:** a String conjunct is evaluated on the dictionary, once per
  value (step 193, `dictfilter.go`): a batch's text columns are marked by index and
  copied only where kept, and its other columns are decoded whole and taken to the
  kept rows (`compact`, `dictfilter.go:356`, 7% of q12). A date's range, an
  integer's or a decimal's comparison is evaluated on every decoded row.

**Beyond the scan:** q16's group-by of a brand, a type and a size is the generic key
table's (`keytable.go:306`, 22%); h2o's joins copy their text payloads to the output
(`takeInto`, `column.go:474`, with `memmove`, a fifth of `j1`); h2o `gb4` is a quarter
zstd's Huffman stage.

## 2. The Parquet read

The suites' largest share, and the one the most queries share.

### R1. Buffers that are reused

- **A pooled allocator** for the page readers' buffers, so a row group's pages are
  decompressed into the last one's, not into fresh zeroed memory.
- **The decoded columns' buffers** sized once a column and reused where the batch is
  consumed before the next is made, and allocated without zeroing where every byte is
  written.
- **Measured:** the bare scan's GC, zeroing and copying share, from 29%, and its time
  on one thread and four.

### R2. A predicate's columns first, the rest for the rows it keeps

- **Every dictionary-encoded column, not text alone:** a conjunct over one column of a
  date, an integer, a float or a decimal type, row-local as step 193's are, is
  evaluated once per dictionary value, so `l_shipdate`'s range in q14 is a few
  thousand comparisons a row group where it was 124,000. A PLAIN column's conjunct is
  evaluated on its decoded values, before the other columns are.
- **The other columns decoded for the kept rows only:** a PLAIN value gathered from
  the page, a dictionary index looked up only where kept, a string copied only where
  kept, where now each is decoded whole and taken (`compact`). A row group of which
  no row is kept decompresses no other column.
- **The Filter above stays,** as step 193's does: what the reader cannot judge it
  keeps.
- **Measured:** q14, q15, q6, q19, q12 and q1, whose filters are dates, discounts,
  quantities and text, at SF=1 against step R1's runner and Polars; the answers the
  same.

### R3. The decoding itself

- **Dictionary indices unpacked** into the batch's own buffer, eight at a time
  (`rle.go:121`, 10% of q12), and a dictionary's values gathered by them in one
  loop.
- **Strings from a dictionary** gathered without a pass to measure them first
  (`pageStrings.mark` and `emit`, 21% of q12 and 16% of q19).
- **Measured** as R2.

### R4. zstd

No code: the floor is recorded, in `feature-gaps.md` §8, with this step's
measurements, so a later reader does not chase it. A pure-Go decoder of ursus's own is
out of 0.7's scope.

## 3. The operators the suites lean on

### J1. A join's text payload

h2o's joins gather each matched row's text from the build side into the output,
copying its bytes (`takeInto`), and zero the buffer first. Measured on `j1` and
`j3`, and Polars', whose text is a view and is not copied: the gather sized once,
written without zeroing, and, where the probe's matches are in the build's order,
copied in runs.

### G1. A group-by of several keys

The generic key table encodes each row's keys to bytes and hashes them
(`keytable.go:306`). Keys of fixed width together, `p_size` beside two strings, and a
row's hash combined from its columns' where each column is hashed whole. Measured on
q16 and h2o `gb3` and `gb6`.

## 4. `feature-gaps.md` §8's P1 items

Each is measured before it is built, against Polars on the same input.

- **O1. Predicate pushdown through a group-by's keys and a window's partition keys**
  (`rule_predicate.go:174`, a barrier now): a filter on a key column moves below the
  group-by or the window, as Polars moves it.
- **O2. Expression-level CSE:** an expression repeated in a `Select` or a
  `WithColumns`, `x.Mean()` in two outputs, computed once.
- **W1. Windows:** `Over`'s keys hashed a column at a time by the group-by's tables;
  a window in `Agg`, `Agg(Col("x").CumSum())`, computed in the group-by's own groups
  (113 ms where Polars takes 13.5); a window not joined back to its whole input.
- **W2. An expression's rolling:** `Col("x").Sum().Rolling(t)` by a sliding state, 49
  ms where Polars takes 10.
- **C1. The CSV writer on several threads:** 397 ms where Polars takes 168.
- **C2. The CSV reader a column at a time:** what step 373 left, about a seventh of
  the read.
- **U1. `Upsample`** without a join and a sort where its input is sorted: 355 ms where
  Polars takes 24.
- **Q1. Scalar subqueries:** an aggregate a query needs as one value computed once
  before it, as E1's scalars and `IsIn(Col("b").Implode())` want (step 258), where it
  is joined to the rows as a frame of one row now. PDS-H's q11, q15 and q22 hold one.
- **T1. A large top-k that sorts rather than heaps** (step 247): a `Head(k)` after a
  sort keeps a heap of k, which past a k of some thousands costs more than the sort.
- **S1. A work-stealing scheduler** only if the profiles show threads idle: q12 keeps
  3.6 of four busy now. Measured first, each worst query's CPU time over its wall time
  on four and eight threads.

**Considered and left:** join reordering on statistics (§8's P2: the suites' joins
are written in a good order); a text type of views, as Polars' strings are, which
would make a gather copy no bytes but changes every text kernel; and a zstd decoder of
ursus's own.

## 5. The claims

- **No answer changes,** to the bit where it is a float; the 22 PDS-H answers match
  DuckDB's at every step's gate.
- **Each item is measured** before and after, against the previous step's runner,
  alternating, and against Polars on the same threads.
- **No regression:** a step that changes what the suites run is timed on PDS-H at
  SF=0.1 against the previous runner, as the reports have been.
- **The plans** change only where O1 and O2 change them, each changed plan listed and
  checked by hand.
- **Each step:** tests and teeth that bite; the light gate, one package at a time;
  CI's every width, scalar build and race detector after the push.
