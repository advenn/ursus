    Changelog¶

    ursus is on ZeroVer: 0.x, and a stable 1.0 is not expected yet. Go's rule for v0 applies, so a minor version may change behaviour, and an API may be renamed, removed or reshaped when the result is better, without being deprecated first. Each release below therefore says what a user of the previous one can trip on, not only what is new; from 0.6 on, each lists its breaks with their replacements.

    Every step of construction has an as-built record in context_files/, with the measurements behind each change. Step numbers below point at those records.

    Unreleased — toward v0.7¶

    Everything since v0.6.8 (2026-10-26): steps 375–389, so far. v0.7-scope.md set the scope: performance, the Parquet read first.

    Faster¶

    • A CSV file's floats are read in one pass however they are written (389): a float of 17 digits, as Go and Polars write one in full, was parsed three times; its value is now found by the Eisel-Lemire algorithm, as strconv finds it, and its fraction read eight digits at a time. Such a float parses in a third of its time, and a Cast(String) to a float with it.
    • Collect copies its answer on the query's threads (389): a result's batches were concatenated into one frame on one thread after the query ran on all of them; each column's rows are now copied in pieces at once. A million rows read from a CSV file on four threads take 0.78 of their time.
    • An aggregate over a window no longer copies the frame (388): x - x.Mean() or x.Sum().Over(g) in a WithColumns hands its batches on as they came, each with the window's values, where the window concatenated them all first.
    • Windows in one WithColumns or Select are computed at once (387), a thread each, where they were one after another: a rolling mean, max and std over a million rows take 0.55 of their time. An integer rolling sum reads its values in place.
    • A window function in an aggregation runs over each group's list (386): GroupBy(g).Agg(Col("x").CumSum()) collects each group's values and runs the window over them, where it partitioned the rows a second time; a million rows of a thousand groups take 0.52 of their time. List().Eval of a window with no order takes half its time, and an integer CumSum reads its values in place.
    • A large top-k sorts rather than heaps (385): a Head(k) after a Sort kept a heap of k rows, whose cost grows past a sort's for a k of some tens of thousands; past that it sorts every row and keeps k. The first million of ten million floats take 327–357 ms where they took 3,994–4,202.
    • Upsample no longer sorts its answer (383): its grid is joined to the frame keeping the grid's order, where both were numbered and the join sorted back. A million rows of 100 groups upsampled by the second take 0.74 of their time.
    • A filter of a group-by's keys runs before it (382): GroupBy(k).Agg(...) filtered on k filters k first, through a key's definition, and a filter of a window's partition keys runs before the window; a filter of an aggregate, HAVING, stays after, as does one of a float key. Over ten million rows of a thousand keys filtered to ten, the group-by takes 12 ms where it took 52, and a window 14 where it took 197.
    • A group-by of several integer keys packs them (381, 384): where they fit two words with a null bit each, as two Int32s do, or three, as two Int64s do, a row's keys are hashed and compared as integers, where they were encoded to bytes; and NUnique of an integer column keys its values so too. h2o's gb6 takes 0.85 to 0.93 of its time, PDS-H's q16 0.89 to 0.94 and q20 0.96.
    • A join gathers its text by runs (379): a run of consecutive matched rows, as a probe side's are in order, is copied at once, and a short string alone is moved as two words, where each row's bytes were a copy of their own. The gather takes 0.53 to 0.60 of its time on a join's two sides; h2o's joins over Parquet 0.96 to 0.99 of theirs.
    • A Parquet scan filtered by text reads its other columns at the kept rows (377): where the reader judges a text's conjunct on its dictionary, as since 0.6.1, the batch's other columns are read only at the rows it keeps, where every row was decoded and the kept ones taken; and the text itself is marked and copied by the kept rows alone (378). PDS-H's q10 and q19 take 0.82 and 0.84 of their time at SF=1, and q12 0.92 of that.
    • A Parquet file's pages are decompressed into reused buffers (376): each column chunk's page reader allocated its buffers afresh, a megabyte a page of DuckDB's lineitem, and the collector and the zeroing followed. A scan of four lineitem columns at SF=1 allocates 2.2 GB in seven runs where it allocated 3.9; PDS-H at SF=0.1 takes 0.93 of v0.6.8's time, and at SF=1 its q14, q17, q19, q12 and q15 0.91 to 0.95.

    v0.6.8 — 2026-10-26¶

    Everything since v0.6.7 (2026-10-26): steps 365–374. v0.6.8-scope.md set the scope: the leftovers of feature completeness, and five of 0.7's performance tasks done ahead of it. Step 374 checked the whole.

    Where it ends¶

    • The leftovers of feature completeness:
    • a database written, WriteDatabase, Polars' write_database, for SQLite, PostgreSQL, MySQL and SQL Server;
    • partitioned writes for the four sinks, Polars' tree name for name;
    • a String or Binary column past 2 GiB, exported to Arrow as the large type.
    • Five of 0.7's performance tasks:
    • Profile, Polars' profile();
    • a count that reads no column: Count over PDS-H's lineitem at SF=1 in 2.5 ms where it took 400 to 500;
    • slice pushdown through what keeps rows: a projected sort's head over lineitem a top-k, 147 ms where it took 380;
    • a frame's aggregate that takes its columns whole: Len over a million rows 0.13 ms where it took 2.5;
    • a CSV file read in about 1.4 times Polars' time, where it was 2.4, and h2o's gb1 over CSV in 0.78 of its time.
    • Each answers as Polars 1.44.1 does, checked against its answers on the same inputs, but for the differences feature-gaps.md §9 records.
    • Nothing the benchmarks run got slower:
    • the optimized plans of the 22 PDS-H and 15 h2o queries are byte-identical to v0.6.7's;
    • the strings, the scans, the aggregates and the CSV reader changed beneath them, so PDS-H at SF=0.1 was run against v0.6.7's, alternating: 1.00 of its time in all, twice, every query within its noise;
    • every PDS-H answer at SF=0.1, at every step, and every h2o answer at 10,000,000 rows, over Parquet and over CSV, match DuckDB's.

    Breaks¶

    • An empty hive key of text is the empty string (367), as Polars reads k=, where it was null; in a key of any other type it is null still.
    • Count computes no column it does not need (370): a query's rows are counted through a projection of none, so a column that would fail to compute, or a udf's that nothing reads, no longer runs, and no longer fails or acts under a count. A filter's columns still do.
    • SinkNDJSON takes NDJSONSinkOptions (367): a CollectOption, as before, or a PartitionOption. A call passing a []CollectOption spread with ... needs its slice's type changed.

    Features¶

    • Profile (369), Polars' profile(): a query's answer and a frame of each node of its plan, as Explain prints it, after the optimization's row: its start and end in microseconds, as Polars', and its rows, batches, own time, summed over the threads that did it, and peak_memory. Collect builds none of its wrappers. ExplainOptFlags(f) renders the plan WithOptFlags(f) runs.

    • Large strings (368): a String or Binary column past 2 GiB of characters holds 64-bit offsets, where it was refused, so a Collect, a sort, a join, a group-by or a window over more than 2 GiB of text answers. It exports to Arrow as a LargeString or a LargeBinary, a slice of it that fits as a String; ScanArrowRecords reads such a record back, the IPC writers split a batch that would be one, and it spills. A batch a source reads keeps 32-bit offsets and refuses past them, saying to read it in smaller batches.

    • Partitioned writes (367): WithPartitionBy(keys...) makes SinkParquet, SinkCSV, SinkIPC and SinkNDJSON write a hive dataset, Polars' write_parquet(partition_by=...) and sink_parquet(pl.PartitionBy(...)): a directory a key's value, its files numbered from 00000000, the values escaped as Polars escapes them and a null __HIVE_DEFAULT_PARTITION__, so the tree is Polars' name for name and each reads the other's; WithPartitionIncludeKey(false) drops the keys from the files, and WithMaxRowsPerFile(n) ends a file at n rows, with or without keys. Streamed, a file written to a temporary name and renamed as it ends.

    • A database written (366): WriteDatabase, Polars' write_database, on a LazyFrame or a DataFrame, through database/sql into a *sql.DB, a *sql.Conn or a *sql.Tx; WithIfTableExists(IfExistsFail | IfExistsReplace | IfExistsAppend) as Polars' modes; a table created of the types ReadDatabase reads back, for SQLite, PostgreSQL, MySQL or SQL Server, named by the driver or WithDialect; the rows in INSERTs of as many as the dialect's parameters allow, in one transaction.

    Faster¶

    • A CSV file read faster (373): a float parsed in one pass, to strconv's bits, and an integer; a batch's column with no null has no validity bitmap, so the kernels after the read take their paths for none; an unquoted field's end found in a mask of 64 bytes. A million rows of an integer, two texts and a float, 43 MB, in 165–250 ms on one thread where they took 243–405, 78–142 on four where they took 133–188, at a load of 4 to 5.7; Polars 111–146 and 55–68. A cast from String to a float takes the same parse.
    • A frame's aggregate takes its columns whole (372): an aggregate of no keys, Select(c("x").Mean()), makes no group id a row; a count, a sum, a mean, a minimum and a maximum, a first and a last take each batch's column at once, an integer's sum and mean exact in 128 bits without a copy, a float's in the same order, to the bit. Over a million rows Len takes 0.13 ms where it took 2.5, an integer's mean 1.0 where it took 3.8; Select(Len()) over lineitem at SF=1 2.3 ms where it took 30.
    • Slice pushdown (371): a Head or a Slice tells the scan or the sort beneath it how few rows it needs through a projection, a WithColumns, a row index, a union's inputs and an ordered left join's left input, where it reached only the one directly beneath it. A projected sort's head over lineitem at SF=1 is a top-k, 147 ms where it took 380.
    • A count reads no column (370): Count and Select(Len()) scan none of a frame's columns, where they read all, a Parquet file's rows its footers', no column chunk opened. Over PDS-H's lineitem at SF=1, Count takes 2.5 ms where it took 400–500; Select(Len()) 27–39, the rest its aggregation's.

    Known limitations¶

    As v0.6.7's, except:

    • Lifted: a database written, partitioned writes and a String column of more than 2 GiB (366–368); a count that reads every column (370).
    • Not built, in polars-parity.md: Excel, Avro, Delta and Iceberg, and a plan serialized.
    • A batch a source reads holds at most 2 GiB of text a column (368): a Parquet column chunk's, a CSV block's or one Arrow record's rows. Past it the read refuses, saying to read it in smaller batches; a whole frame past it is held.
    • A CSV file is read in about 1.4 times Polars' time (373), and the CSV writer runs on one thread.

    v0.6.7 — 2026-10-26¶

    Everything since v0.6.6 (2026-10-19): steps 288–364. v0.6.7-scope.md set the scope: feature completeness, part 3 — frame operations. It took in what was to be 0.6.8's I/O and the types after it, and three steps of performance. Step 364 checked the whole.

    Where it ends¶

    • Polars' frame operations:
    • rows: UniqueKeep, duplicates over columns, samples, negative slices, the row shortcuts;
    • columns and types: a frame's cast, MatchToSchema, a frame's aggregates and fills, InsertColumn, HashRows;
    • reshaping: a lazy Pivot, Unpivot without On, PartitionBy, Upsample, Concat's modes, Transpose named from a column, ToDummies;
    • group-bys: a group's Head and Tail, Having, FirstNonNull, the groups as frames;
    • joins: maintain_order, an as-of numeric tolerance and coalesce, Update;
    • Go values in and out: FromRows, Rows, ToMaps, IterRows, a Series with operations.
    • What remained of Polars' API, by a diff of the two (polars-parity.md): selectors, the name and meta namespaces, TopKBy, InterpolateBy, an expression's rolling, a window inside a window, a list a group in Agg.
    • Types: Array and the Arr() namespace, Categorical, a List, an Array or a Struct as a key.
    • I/O: Arrow IPC, JSON and NDJSON read and written; compressed input; Parquet, CSV, NDJSON and IPC directories, globs and hive partitions; Parquet schema evolution; databases read through database/sql; the rest of read_csv's and write_csv's options, and Polars' date patterns.
    • Performance: a sort and a unique on several threads, and text sorted by its bytes: a million distinct integers sort in 30 ms on eight threads where they took 100, and their unique takes 54 to 105 ms where it took 430 to 640.
    • Each answers as Polars 1.44.1 does, checked against its answers on the same inputs, but for the differences feature-gaps.md §9 records.
    • Nothing the benchmarks run got slower:
    • the optimized plans of the 22 PDS-H and 15 h2o queries are byte-identical to v0.6.6's;
    • the sort changed beneath them, so PDS-H at SF=0.1 was run against v0.6.6's, alternating: 0.95 to 0.98 of its time in all, every query within its noise;
    • every PDS-H answer at SF=0.1, at every step, and every h2o answer at 10,000,000 rows over Parquet match DuckDB's.

    Breaks¶

    • A CSV record of too few fields is padded with nulls, and a blank line read as a record of nulls (357), as Polars reads them: the record was refused unless WithTruncateRaggedLines(true), and the line skipped in a file of more than one column. WithTruncateRaggedLines now concerns a record of too many fields alone.
    • A CSV file's text that is not UTF-8 is refused (357), as Polars refuses it, where a String held its bytes as they were, which no text kernel reads rightly. WithEncoding(ursus.UTF8Lossy) reads each invalid sequence as U+FFFD, and a column's name is read so whatever the encoding.
    • Str().ToDatetime(unit, tz) reads a text of no offset on tz's wall clock (356), as Polars' str.to_datetime(time_zone=tz) does: 2024-01-02 03:04:05 in Tokyo is 03:04:05 there, where it was a cast's, 03:04:05 UTC, 12:04:05 in Tokyo. A text with an offset is its instant, as before. .Cast(ursus.Datetime(unit, tz)) reads it as UTC's still, as Polars' CSV reader does.
    • A CSV file's instants are written as Polars writes them (354): a naive one with no Z, 2024-01-02T03:04:05.123456, where it had one, as though it were UTC, and a zoned one with its offset as +0900, where it was +09:00, or Z in UTC. A float is written in Polars' text, the cast's since step 325: 123456789.0 and 1e-7, where they were Go's 1.23456789e+08 and 1e-07. ScanCSV reads both forms.
    • An Arrow dictionary of text is read as a Categorical (352), as Polars reads one, where it was read as a String: an IPC file's, a Parquet file's that its footer says is one, a pandas categorical's. .Cast(ursus.String) gives the text.
    • QCut and Hist's category answer a Categorical (352), as Polars' do, where they answered a String.
    • Cut answers an Enum (350) of its bins' labels, in their order, as Polars' cut does, where it answered a String: .Cast(ursus.String) gives the text. Its labels must differ, as an Enum's categories do, and a break is written in decimal, (-inf, 0.0000001], as Polars writes it, where it was (-inf, 1e-07].
    • A null's rank is null (335), as Polars': Rank ranked a null as the least value and gave it a position, so [5, null, 4] ranked [3, 1, 2]; it ranks [2, null, 1] now, the values among themselves, by every method, in a window and over the frame, streamed past a memory limit and in List().Eval. A rank's column is nullable where its input is.
    • A float cast to text is Polars' text (325): 2.0 is "2.0", -0.0 "-0.0", 1e-7 "1e-7", 1e16 "1e+16" and +Inf "inf", where they were Go's "2", "-0", "1e-07", "1e+16" and "+Inf". A pivot named by a float's text names its column so too, 2.0 where it was 2.
    • An as-of tolerance of zero admits only an exact match (315), as Polars' does: AsOfTolerance(ursus.FromDuration(0)) admitted any distance.
    • An as-of join keeps both ordering keys when they are named apart (315), as Polars' does: AsOfLeftOn(ts) with AsOfRightOn(quote_ts) gives ts and quote_ts, where quote_ts was merged into ts.
    • JoinMaintainOrder takes a JoinOrder (314), Polars' maintain_order, where it took a Boolean: JoinMaintainOrder(true) is JoinMaintainOrder(ursus.JoinOrderLeft).
    • RollingOptions.Offset is where a window starts (313), as Polars' offset: (t + Offset, t + Offset + Period], minus the period when unset, as before. It moved the window's end, so an offset of e is now e less the period: Every("15m") at a period of 2h is Every("-1h45m"). NoOffset() is Polars' offset="0d", a window after its row. A window whose offset is as long as its period, behind its row, ends at the row, as Polars', a month counted as 28 days.
    • A null condition takes Otherwise (311), as Polars' when and SQL's CASE WHEN do: When(c).Then(a).Otherwise(b) of a null c is b, or the next When's, where it was null. A conditional's result is nullable only where a branch is. Clip with a null bound now leaves that side unclipped, as Polars' does, where it gave null; FillNan still leaves a null alone.
    • ConcatMode is ursus's own type (307), no longer the plan's, its String the name of Polars' mode: ConcatStrict prints "vertical", where it printed "strict". Code that names the constants is unchanged.
    • PivotOptions.On is a []string (303), for several columns whose values together name a new column: On: "quarter" is On: []string{"quarter"}.
    • A selection among a variadic function's operands is now its operands, as in Polars (302). SumHorizontal(All()) is each row's sum across every column, one column. Before, the function was repeated a column at a time, so MaxHorizontal(All()) gave each column itself. This holds for the six horizontal reductions, ConcatStr, ConcatStrIgnoreNulls, Struct, ConcatList, List().Concat and Coalesce. Format still formats a column at a time, as Polars' does.
    • FillStrategy is a uint64, where it was a uint8 (301): its bits above the rule carry a limit. A conversion to uint8 no longer compiles, and a strategy with a limit is not == the bare one, FillForward.Limit(1) != FillForward.
    • A slice whose window starts before the first element is cut by as much, as every Polars slice is (291): Str().Slice(-7, 2) of "012345" is "0", and List().Slice(-99, 2) of three elements is none. Both clamped the start to the first element and kept the whole length.

    Fixes¶

    • A date before 1677 or after 2262 as a Go value (355): Rows, ToMaps and At gave a Date or a Datetime of such a year as a wrong instant, its nanosecond count overflowed, 0999-01-02 as 2168-02-10; it is the instant now.

    • A frame collected from several batches says its columns hold no null (321), where they hold none: concatenating the batches built each column a bitmap with every bit set, and every kernel after read it a bit a row.

    • A null instant decodes as the zero time.Time (317), in Rows and CollectInto, as a null decodes as every other field type's zero: it was the epoch, 1970-01-01, a plausible instant no row held. IsZero now tells it.
    • A conditional of Lists or Structs (310): When(...).Then(list).Otherwise(...), and Coalesce of Lists, were refused, "a conditional is not implemented for List(String)"; each row is now its branch's, as Polars' when/then gives.
    • An expression that reads no column is named as its first input is (309), as Polars names it: Len().Cast(Int64) and Len().Add(1) are "len", where they were "literal".
    • A pivot's cell that no value falls in is Agg of none (303), as Polars' is: a count's 0, where every such cell was null. A sum of none stays null, as ursus's every empty sum is.
    • A fold in a list's body (302): List().Eval(Fold(...)) and a Fold in CumulativeEval's body failed, "fold reached typing unexpanded", since folds came in (266). A body is lowered when it is written, before any schema, so a fold or a function across columns in it is now built over its expressions as written.
    • A printed frame aligns text outside ASCII (296). It padded each cell by its bytes, so a row holding "Zoë" came out a column short; it pads by the columns a terminal draws, two for a wide East Asian character and none for a combining mark, as Polars measures them.
    • WithMemoryLimit's doc said the default budget is Linux's alone; it has been macOS's and Windows's too since step 181 (294).
    • The expression Slice in a Select kept a row too many where its window began before the first row, Slice(-7, 2) of six rows two (291); and in an aggregation, a negative length, "to the end", failed.

    Features¶

    • A sort of text by its bytes, seven at a time (363): a String, Binary or Categorical key is radix-sorted, alone or beside other keys, where it went to the comparator, in the comparator's order exactly, ties included. A million distinct strings sort in 150 ms on one thread and 75 on eight, where they took 853 and 274; a million URLs of one prefix in 240 and 126, where they took 1,128 and 370.
    • A unique on several threads, in the group-by's tables (362): an integer key's values, a string key's bytes, any other key encoded, where each key was a Go map's string; a table a thread, a key in the one its hash names, each table's rows inserted in order, so the rows kept are one thread's, each key's first. A million distinct integers take 54 to 105 ms where they took 430 to 640, and 1,000 keys among a million rows 16 to 25 where they took 40 to 58.
    • A sort on several threads (361): the radix passes, the merge sort a String key takes, the rows' places in their batches and the gather of the output each split across the query's threads, in the one-thread sort's order exactly, ties included. A million distinct integers sort in 30 ms on eight threads, where they took 100, and in 69 on one.

    Known limitations¶

    As v0.6.6's, except:

    • Lifted: a window inside a window and an aggregate inside an aggregate (336); CorrSpearman and Mode past a memory limit, which spill (339, 340); .arr (341, 342).
    • Not built, in polars-parity.md: Excel, Avro, Delta and Iceberg; a database written; partitioned writes; a plan serialized; a String column of more than 2 GiB, which is refused. 0.6.8 takes partitioned writes, large strings and a database written.
    • A count still reads every column: Select(Len()) over Parquet projects them all; Polars reads none.
    • A CSV file is read in about three and a half times Polars' time (step 357), and the CSV writer runs on one thread.

    Breaks¶

    • Str().SplitN(by, n) is a Struct, fields field_0 to field_{n-1}, as Polars' splitn (270). It was a List, n <= 0 unlimited: Str().Split(by) is every part.
    • A struct's field is named after the field, as Polars names it (275): Col("s").Struct().Field("b") is b, where it was s. So WithFields of a field read sets that field, where it added one named after the struct.
    • A null bound of Clip is no bound, for its row, as Polars' None is (283). It was a null result, and a null literal bound nulled the whole column.
    • Str() refuses a Binary column, as Polars' .str does (274). It read one as text, and a text function such as Slice split its bytes as runes; Bin() has the byte functions.

    Fixed¶

    Behaviour changes a v0.3 user can trip on¶

    • The Go runtime's soft memory limit is set by default (128). The first query that runs under the default budget sets it to nine tenths of the ceiling the budget is derived from, as SetProcessMemoryLimit does. The limit is the whole process's, so it changes how the program around ursus collects garbage too. GOMEMLIMIT in the environment, off included, a limit already set with debug.SetMemoryLimit, a limit given with WithMemoryLimit, and LeaveProcessMemoryLimit each keep ursus from setting it.

    • Collect refuses a result too large to hold, under the default budget (130). Once the query holds more, its result and its operators' state together, than three quarters of the ceiling, Collect fails with a resource error naming collect and pointing to CollectBatches, SinkParquet and SinkCSV, which stream. It used to grow until the kernel killed the process. Under a limit given with WithMemoryLimit, or none, Collect holds whatever it is asked to, as before.

    • An inner join's row order is no longer promised to follow the left frame: the smaller input is hashed, whichever side it is. JoinMaintainOrder(true) keeps the old order. Left, right, semi, anti and full joins are unchanged (104).

    • Int128 arithmetic is exact or refused. + and - used to wrap, so Max + Max gave a plausible wrong number. A result past ±1.7e38 is now an error naming the row. Int64 arithmetic still wraps, as Polars' does (100).
    • A null in a column declared non-nullable is an error in production, not only in the test suite (98).
    • A median or an implode over heavily overlapping rolling windows is refused under the budget, naming the operator, where it used to grow past it (96).
    • A List column past 2^31-1 elements is refused, naming CollectBatches and SinkParquet (99).
    • A group-by under a limit you set is serial again, as in v0.3.0, so a spilling group-by gives the same order on every run. In v0.3.1 it was parallel until half the budget (92).
    • Parquet: a file with an INT96 column opens, where it failed; a column of the NULL logical type reads as Null, not Int32 (108, 112).
    • Peak memory of a Parquet scan rises by the few batches each worker decodes ahead. WithThreads(1) reads serially, as before (102).
    • Error kinds that changed, for code that tests them with errors.Is:
    • a panic in a MapName function is ErrValue, and a panic in the RecordReader that ScanArrow's factory returns is ErrIO; both were ErrInternal (115);
    • a dynamic group-by over a row at the last instant a time.Time holds is ErrValue, not ErrInternal (115);
    • a failed spill write is ErrIO; it had no kind (115).
    • Error text changed for FillNan, FillNull, CumSum, a Duration scaled by a fraction, a failing udf (111), a window inside Agg, an ordering over a windowed aggregate, and Any (115).

      • over CSV, it peaks at 2.75 GB and allocates 14.9 GB;
      • the heap in use still sits near the soft limit while allocation outpaces the collector.

      This item was corrected on master after the tag: as tagged, it ended at the figures above it.

    v0.3.1 — 2026-10-06¶

    Steps 89–91: what benchmarking v0.3.0 found, fixed.

    • A query is budgeted by default, so it spills under a container's limit.
    • With no WithMemoryLimit, the budget is half the smaller of the cgroup's memory limit (the least over the process's cgroup and its ancestors) and the machine's RAM, on Linux.
    • v0.3.0 spilled only under an explicit limit, and was killed by the kernel under a cgroup's instead: h2o gb10 over CSV, at the benchmark's 8 GB.
    • WithMemoryLimit(0) is unlimited, the old default. Off Linux nothing changes.
    • Behaviour change: an operator that cannot spill now fails at that point, with an error naming itself, rather than running into swap (90).
    • A group-by stays parallel under a budget.
    • It runs on several workers until the query holds half its budget, then folds them into one and finishes serially, spilling as before.
    • In v0.3.0 any limit made it serial outright, which with a default budget would have made every group-by serial: about 1.5× slower on h2o's small ones, measured (91).
    • PDS-H q7 is fixed: 1.55 s and 0.59 GB, against 3.9 s and 1.68 GB in v0.3.0. A temporal cast that cannot fail, such as a nanosecond Datetime narrowed to a Date, no longer keeps a filter above the joins it should be pushed into (89).

    v0.3.0 — 2026-10-06¶

    Everything since v0.2.0 (2026-09-04): steps 22–88, about 270 commits.

    v0.3-scope.md set the scope: the types and the I/O are honest. Most of the release is correctness work. A measured audit, audit.md, found 78 defects, about 40 of them silent wrong answers. Steps 70–85 fixed nearly all of them, each alongside the instrument that would have caught it.

    Highlights¶

    • Nested data, end to end.
    • List and Struct columns read from Parquet (steps 28–34) and Arrow (60), and write to Parquet (81). pyarrow and Polars read ursus's nested files value for value.
    • A file holding a nested column ursus cannot read no longer refuses as a whole (28).
    • Explode (30), Unnest (35), Implode (46) and Unpivot (47).
    • A .list namespace of seventeen methods (32, 33), and .Struct().Field() (34).
    • Str().Split, SplitN and ExtractAll, which build lists (45).
    • Decimal you can compute with.
    • sum is an exact Decimal(38, s); mean, var, std, median, quantile and product are Float64 (69).
    • + - * are exact and refused past 38 digits; / is the nearest Float64 (80).
    • Every cast between Decimal and a number is exact or refused (69).
    • Decimals of different scales compare, join and concatenate exactly (80).
    • Enum you can build: cast from String, strict or lossy. Sort, group-by, join, unique and windows run in category order, and CSV reads and writes it (79).
    • Joins:
    • JoinWhere for non-equi joins (43);
    • WhereExists and WhereNotExists (44), which made PDS-H q21 1.4× faster with 1.75× less memory;
    • a parallel join probe (36);
    • keys of different types meeting at a type that holds both (74).
    • UDFs: MapElements per value and MapBatches per column, Go functions that take part in the plan (50).
    • Arrow both ways: df.Record(), df.ArrowSchema() and lf.CollectRecords() export without copying (58); ScanArrow and ScanArrowRecords import (60).
    • Spilling that covers what people hit.
    • Unique and windows with a partition key spill, and keep their order exactly.
    • Sort, group-by and join spill frames that carry nested columns.
    • Every operator that cannot spill fails with an error naming itself (82).
    • Reading from anywhere:
    • ScanParquetFrom reads Parquet through any io.ReaderAt, and ScanCSVFrom CSV through any stream, each Open given the query's context (83).
    • A ranged GET reaches an object store, and ursus fetches only the footer and the column chunks a query needs.
    • Performance:
    • The CSV reader makes 126× fewer allocations, and is about 2× faster (22).
    • Groups are found through an open-addressed key table (23).
    • The radix sort is 2.4–4.1× faster (24).
    • In-memory frames honour the batch size, so the join is 3.7× faster at eight threads (36, 37).
    • CSV columns convert in parallel (42).
    • PDS-H SF=1's peak memory fell from 3.98 to 2.26 GB (38–41).

    Behaviour changes a v0.2 user can trip on¶

    Most of these were wrong answers. A few were answers that are now refused, because the right one could not be given.

    Types and casts

    • A literal keeps its type: i8 + int8(100) wraps in Int8, and i8 + int64(100) widens. The two used to collide and give one answer (72).
    • String → integer is exact at every width, not through a float64: "9007199254740993" stays itself, and an out-of-range value is refused by Cast (73).
    • To a float: a cast rounds to the nearest. 0.1 → Float32 used to be refused or null. Float32 maths returns the nearest float32 instead of null (73).
    • Between integers: Int64↔Uint64 and Int128→Int64 casts are exact. Sum().Cast(Int64) is exact above 2^53. A strict float → Int128 refuses a fraction (69).
    • Strings that are not numbers: 1_000 and 0x1p3 are no longer parsed as numbers, in Cast, in CSV inference, or in float schemas (77).
    • Datetime → Time takes the time of day. It used to reinterpret the epoch ticks (57).
    • Int64 → Time refuses a value outside the day when strict, and is null when lossy, as Polars does; it used to fold 90000 seconds to 01:00 (86).
    • A Duration cast to a coarser unit truncates toward zero, as Polars and TotalSeconds do: −1.5 s is −1 s, where it was −2 s. An instant still floors (86).
    • Instants outside 1678–2262: they are null in Values([]time.Time), and refused by Lit and by casts from String. They used to wrap: 2300 became 1715 (61).
    • Datetime → Date errors when strict and is null when lossy (49).
    • Refused at plan time: Bool ↔ temporal casts (52), and == on a List or a Struct (64).
    • Enum:
    • Enum → number goes through its text;
    • number → Enum is refused;
    • an Enum and a String meet at String in Concat, joins and conditionals (79).

    Arithmetic and functions

    • Decimal results have new types (80):
    • + - give Decimal(max integer digits + max scale + 1, max scale), so Decimal(10,2) + Decimal(10,2) is Decimal(11,2);
    • * gives Decimal(p1+p2, s1+s2);
    • each is refused past 38 digits;
    • / returns Float64;
    • Decimal with a float is Float64, and Decimal with an Int128 is refused.
    • Temporal arithmetic is checked, not wrapped.
    • An overflowing Datetime ± Duration, Datetime − Datetime or Duration arithmetic is a KindValue refusal naming the row (61).
    • Plain integer arithmetic still wraps, as it does in Polars.
    • Date − Date is a Duration, and Date ± Duration a Datetime. Naive minus UTC is refused (52).
    • Time ± Duration wraps around midnight (57).
    • Abs and Neg of the minimum Duration are refused; they wrapped (86).
    • FloorDiv floors and Mod takes the divisor's sign, for signed integers, floats and Durations (54, 76). A float % follows Python, with ±Inf and the signed zero.
    • Diff on an unsigned column widens: UInt8→Int16, UInt16→Int32, UInt32→Int64 and UInt64→Int128. It used to wrap: UInt8 3 then 1 was 254 (85).
    • Dt().Epoch() floors, and it and Total* are exact past 2262 (61, 76).
    • Truncate and GroupByDynamic floor the wall clock, not the UTC grid, which moves answers in zones like +05:30. A truncated instant outside the type is refused (76).
    • IsIn and list.contains answer what Eq answers. A type == would refuse is refused at plan time (76).
    • String functions (76):
    • a first-match regex Replace expands $1 and ${name};
    • SplitN(0) is unlimited;
    • CountMatches("") counts;
    • the one-sided strips trim Unicode whitespace.
    • Intervals:
    • an Every that overflows sets Err();
    • IntervalOf refuses mixed signs (66);
    • an interval finer than its index's resolution is refused (68).
    • PadStart, PadEnd and ZFill refuse a width whose output a String column cannot hold. It used to be a fatal out-of-memory (85).
    • A String column past 2 GiB of characters is refused, with ErrResource. Its 32-bit offsets used to wrap silently, and Collect reaches that size at around 30 million rows of 100-byte strings. CollectBatches and the sinks never build one column of all of it (87).
    • A group-by or join over more than 2 GiB of distinct keys groups correctly. Its key table's offsets wrapped too (87).

    Aggregations and windows

    • Integer mean divides an exact sum (75).
    • Int128 sum is refused when the total does not fit (69).
    • Duration sum, mean and cum_sum are exact (62).
    • quantile at an exact rank is that value (75).
    • A variance is never negative (75).
    • PctChange computes in float (75).
    • A Bool sum counts its trues (75).
    • A global GroupBy().Agg() with nothing to aggregate is one row with no columns, where it was shape (0, 0) (75).
    • Undeclared enum values — Rank(99), Interpolation(99), Closed(99) — are KindValue errors. They used to crash, or quietly act as a default (72, 75).
    • A window reads the columns defined before it in the same WithColumns (75).
    • A dynamic group-by's grid covers every row, or refuses at a named ceiling. It used to answer short (67).
    • Two UDFs sharing a name are refused. They used to merge into one computation (65).

    Joins (74)

    • Keys of different types are cast to a type that holds both, strictly. A 64-bit integer key against a float key is refused, and so is Concat or Unpivot of such columns.
    • AsOfBy keys of different integer widths match now; a null by-key matches nothing.
    • Validate 1:m and 1:1 refuse a duplicate key even when it has no match.
    • The as-of tolerance compares in the key's own unit (63).

    I/O

    • Several Parquet or CSV files are matched by column name. A file that differs is refused, naming both. Before, a different column order swapped values silently (71).
    • Row groups are pruned only on what their statistics prove, so some files may read more row groups (71).
    • CSV (77):
    • an unquoted empty field in a String column is null, and a quoted "" is the empty string; the writer quotes every empty string;
    • floats are written so they read back as floats (1.0, inf, NaN);
    • an integer past Int64 infers as Int128;
    • Int128 and Decimal read exactly;
    • an offset with seconds is written in UTC;
    • a blank line in a one-column file is a null;
    • the writers refuse a frame with rows and no columns.
    • Parquet:
    • lists of every element type ursus writes are read (72, 81);
    • WithCompression(Lz4) is refused (use Lz4Raw);
    • a corrupt file is ErrIO rather than a crash or a hang (72).
    • A file that fails to open or read is named in the error (83).

    The optimizer (70, 78)

    • No rule changes an answer any more; an on/off differential over 1629 generated queries agrees.
    • A filter that can fail — a strict cast, a UDF, temporal arithmetic — is no longer moved ahead of a guard, so such a query may read more rows.
    • The planner refuses what Collect would refuse, so some errors arrive at plan time.

    Memory limits (82)

    • A window and an as-of join are charged for the second copy they make.
    • A window now spills at about half the input it used to.
    • An as-of join, or a window with no partition key, now refuses at about half its old size.
    • A group-by is charged for its key table, so it may freeze sooner. Under a limit, the row order of an unordered group-by moves, as documented.

    Errors and testing

    • A panic never kills the host (72).
    • A panicking UDF is the caller's ErrValue.
    • A corrupt Parquet file, or a failing ScanArrow factory, is ErrIO.
    • A UDF that calls runtime.Goexit — as t.FailNow does — is an error. It used to end the stream early, silently (85).
    • ursustest.AssertFrameEqual now compares List and Struct values, and Float32 under a tolerance. Tests that passed may now fail: before, any two nested frames compared equal (60).

    API¶

    Every addition is in the root package unless named.

    • Frames:
    • DataFrame and LazyFrame gain JoinWhere, WhereExists, WhereNotExists and Unpivot (with UnpivotOptions).
    • LazyFrame also gains Explode, Unnest and CollectRecords.
    • DataFrame also gains Record and ArrowSchema.
    • Expressions: Expr.Explode, Expr.Implode, Expr.Unnest, Expr.MapElements and Expr.MapBatches.
    • Namespaces:
    • Expr.List returns a ListExpr, with Len, Get, First, Last, Contains, Min, Max, Sum, Mean, Reverse, Head, Tail, Slice, Sort, SortDesc, Unique and DropNulls.
    • Expr.Struct returns a StructExpr, with Field.
    • StrExpr gains Split, SplitN, ExtractAll, PadStart, PadEnd, ZFill, StripCharsStart, StripCharsEnd and EscapeRegex.
    • Sources: ScanArrow, ScanArrowRecords, ScanParquetFrom with ParquetFile, and ScanCSVFrom with CSVFile.
    • dtype:
    • Added: MaxDecimalPrecision, ValidDecimal, DecimalDigits, DecimalMeet, PromoteExact, ExactMismatch, MeetHint, ParseFloat, TicksPerDay and DataType.FromTimeBound.
    • Removed: DaysInterval and MonthsInterval, which built intervals nothing validated (66). Use IntervalOf.
    • Changed behaviour: Every, IntervalOf and FromDuration validate.
    • i128:
    • Added: ParseDecimal, Int128.MulChecked, Int128.DivUint64 and Int128.Float32.
    • Changed behaviour: Parse checks its range, and Float64 rounds correctly.
    • Nothing else was removed or changed its parameters. Some methods gained named results, which callers cannot see.

    Known limitations¶

    • Object stores: there is no built-in client. S3, GCS and Azure each want a vendor SDK, and ursus is pure Go with one dependency. ScanParquetFrom and ScanCSVFrom are the seam. The stores were planned for 0.4, and have since been deferred past it (v0.4-scope.md §4).
    • Operators that do not spill each fail under WithMemoryLimit with an error naming themselves:
    • reverse, hstack, tail, JoinAsOf, MergeSorted, Rolling and GroupByDynamic;
    • a window with no partition key, and one partition larger than the limit;
    • a cross join;
    • quantile-like aggregates over a few very large groups.
    • Not built: SQL, Pivot, MapGroups, common subexpression elimination, Expr.Rolling*, Upsample, Interpolate, and the trigonometric functions.
    • Types:
    • Nesting deeper than a List of a primitive, or a Struct of primitives, is refused by name in Parquet, both ways.
    • Array has no column representation.
    • CSV refuses nested columns.
    • Enum is not written to Parquet or Arrow.
    • Duration is not written to Parquet; cast it to Int64.
    • Still-open audit rows that can answer wrongly, each recorded in audit.md:
    • I23: a third-party Parquet file written without null counts can be pruned wrongly by an IsNull filter.
    • O13, unmeasured: two Enum or Struct types can render alike.
    • J8, a remainder: an Int64 compared with a float literal past 2^53 meets at Float64.
    • S26: MinInt // -1 wraps.
    • I24: a null in a non-null column is checked only in test builds.
    • Where ursus differs from Polars on purpose:
    • Round rounds half away from zero, which is Polars' mode="half_away_from_zero", not its default, half to even.
    • UInt64 Diff is an exact Int128, where Polars gives Int64 and nulls.
    • The minimum Duration's Abs is refused, where Polars wraps.
    • Speed: ursus is slower than Polars and DuckDB, and the README's table, measured at v0.3.0, says by how much.
    • PDS-H q7 regressed to 3.9 s from 2.4 s, because its date filter is no longer pushed below its joins. Fixed in v0.3.1.
    • ursus spills only under an explicit WithMemoryLimit. Under a cgroup's limit it can be killed instead: h2o gb10 over CSV was, at 8 GB. Fixed in v0.3.1, which budgets every query by default.
